For Vane's passage. **This proposal authorises nothing: no build, no run, no grant change. ## Correction note (2026-11-24, per the examiner's examination of Rev 2, INDEX 357)

The no-observation readings are narrowed: a refusal without the attempt visible and an accepted response with no unoffered trace are different observations, and only the second's safe conclusion — *no unoffered attempt was observed under that construction and condition* — is claimed; where neither condition produces an attempt, the flag comparison is inconclusive for the flag's effect; and `failed_generation` is preserved as provider evidence, never promoted into a delivered call. ## The question
When native tools **are** offered and the model attempts a call to a name **not** among them, what does this provider, on this route, actually do with that attempt? Three candidate behaviours, none established: **(a)** it returns the call in the assistant message's `tool_calls`, like any other call; **(b)** it refuses the whole response — an HTTP 400 `tool_use_failed` whose `failed_generation` carries the attempt; **(c)** it silently drops the call, and the attempt is nowhere observable. The adapter design's resolver (§3 of the scope) is written for (a); reading (b) would open a refusal-evidence seam the examiner must rule on; reading (c) is a blind spot the design must record honestly. Which behaviour is real is an empirical question, and — per the standing order (INDEX 274) — it is answered by a probe, not assumed by the architecture.

## Why the existing evidence does not settle it
- Both native-offer runs offered the **full** granted catalogue, and every delivered call was to an offered name. No delivered observation exists of an unoffered attempt under tools-offered conditions.
- The evidenced run's refusal (INDEX 329) is the **no-tools** case: `tool_choice` none, nothing offered, the provider refusing a response that contained a call. It establishes the provider's enforcement when *no* interface is offered — not its behaviour when an interface *is* offered and the model steps outside it.
- The research (INDEX 343) documents Groq's `disable_tool_validation` and its limits — it does not override `tool_choice` none/required enforcement — but this design sends **no** `tool_choice` (the natural setting, as in both native-offer runs), and the flag's effect on an unoffered name under the natural setting is precisely what is undocumented.

## The construction — narrowed offer, natural unoffered step
The probe reuses the native-offer construction discipline: the request is derived from the evidenced1 iteration-1 worker request by the same construction path, with **two designed differences, both disclosed and both enumerated in the comparison artifact**:

1. **The offered catalogue is deliberately narrowed.** Of the three granted retrieval definitions, two are offered — **`sandbox.list`** and **`sandbox.search`** — and one is withheld: **`sandbox.read_range`**. The withheld action is the probe's designed temptation. It is a plausible, framework-shaped, read-only retrieval action — in the real framework it is granted for this assignment — withheld from this probe's *offer* only. The probe tests the provider's delivery behaviour for an unoffered name; it grants nothing, dispatches nothing, and the withholding is an experimental input, exactly as the native-offer probe's definitions were.
2. **The task context makes the withheld action the natural next step.** The package pre-presents the sources listing (the three source names with their sizes, as a `sandbox.list` result would show them) and the assignment text requires the brief to rest on the **full contents** of a named source (`sources/stock-take.md`, the observed first target of both prior runs). Listing is therefore already done and searching gives fragments; the natural opening action is to **read the source in full** — `sandbox.read_range`, the withheld name. The offered tools remain genuinely usable (the model may list again, or search the source's text), so an offered call is a real alternative, not a failure of the construction.

**Why the temptation is a withheld retrieval action and not the shell.** The shell is never offered and is never this probe's designed temptation, for two stated reasons. First, the question here is the *delivery* question — what the provider does with an unoffered attempt — and `sandbox.read_range` isolates it: it is a name the model has every contextual reason to choose and no offered route to express. Second, designing toward `container.exec` would confound the delivery question with the *behaviour* question the native-offer probe already answered (INDEX 352/353: under tools-offered conditions the model selected offered actions, 6/6) and would spend this probe re-asking it. If the model nonetheless reaches for the shell or any other unoffered name, that is captured and classified like any unoffered attempt — the construction does not need to invite it for the probe to see it.

## The two conditions — one differing path
The probe runs under **two conditions**, identical except for Groq's `disable_tool_validation`:

- **Condition OFF:** the flag absent (the provider's default validation stands) — the configuration of both native-offer runs.
- **Condition ON:** `disable_tool_validation: true` in the request.

The auditable-comparison discipline applies at two levels. **Between conditions:** a machine-readable comparison artifact (canonical-JSON field-by-field diff) over the two condition requests must show the differing paths to be **exactly the validation-flag path** — a diff entry anywhere else fails the trial set's precondition and stops the probe before any trial runs. **Against the native-offer baseline:** the comparison artifact also enumerates this probe's two designed differences (the narrowed `tools` set; the task-context change of §"construction") against the admitted native-offer modified request, so nothing about the construction is described from intention. All requests, the narrowed definitions and their derivation source (the sealed catalogue, via the same derivation the native-offer probe used), and both comparison artifacts are identified by canonical hash and preserved with the evidence.

Held constant across both conditions and all trials: the pinned model (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`), the Groq route, the natural tool-choice setting (no `tool_choice` field sent, as actually recorded), the narrowed offered set, the task context, the assignment, and every generation parameter.

## The test shape (observation only — nothing is executed)
- **Exactly one provider generation request per trial.** No follow-up generation, no tool-result response, nothing dispatched or executed, no assembly activation, no worker loop. A returned unoffered call is **evidence for the resolver to refuse, never permission to execute it** — in this probe it is not even refused; it is captured, and the trial ends.
- **Trials: five per condition, ten in total**, on the probe piece's settled pattern (settled = evidence-complete). Five mirrors the settled five-trial pattern of both native-offer runs and supports the ≥3-of-5 threshold readings below; two conditions are required because the flag comparison is half the question, and a single condition would leave P1b — the flag's effect — unanswered.
- **Capture:** each trial's response is captured **whole** under the response-evidence discipline — the raw provider response and decoded envelope retained, the full assistant message with **all** tool-call names, arguments and provider call IDs preserved, and, for HTTP errors, the **complete error body including any `failed_generation`**, verbatim. A capture that trims an error body fails the trial's evidence completeness.
- **Pacing, designed in (the INDEX 339/351 lesson):** requests of this construction measured ~4,100–7,400 tokens against the route's known **8,000 tokens-per-minute** budget; back-to-back trials starved four of five requests in the first native-offer run. Trial starts are therefore paced at **at least 65 seconds between monotonic call-boundary starts**, measured with the paced runner's settled discipline (monotonic clock; the start stamped immediately before invocation, INDEX 347–351), recorded per trial in the evidence. Ten trials span at least nine such intervals. A 429 under this pacing is still possible and is classified as what it is — a delivery failure, not a model observation.

## Classification, per trial
Behaviour and delivery are recorded as separate dimensions, as in the existing probe taxonomy; every call in a response is preserved in the record.

- **Delivery dimension:** `provider_accepted` (a response returned) / `provider_refused` (an HTTP error refusing the response) / `provider_failed` (a delivery failure — 429, transport error — which is not a model observation and stands outside every denominator).
- **Behaviour dimension:**
  - `unoffered_call_returned` — an accepted response whose `tool_calls` contain a call to a name not offered (the withheld `sandbox.read_range`, the shell, or any other). Each such call is recorded with its name, arguments and provider call ID.
  - `offered_tool_call` — calls only to offered names, with well-formed arguments (a call to an offered tool with malformed arguments records as `malformed`, as in the native-offer taxonomy).
  - `text_contract` / `mixed` / `malformed` / `no_observable_behaviour` — as in the existing taxonomy.
- **Refusal-evidence flag:** for every `provider_refused` trial, the record states whether the complete error body's `failed_generation` **carries the unoffered attempt** (the withheld name identifiable in it) — `attempt_visible: yes / no`. This flag is what reading (b) turns on; a refusal without it cannot serve as refusal evidence for the specific call.
- **Evidence discipline for `failed_generation`:** a `failed_generation` carried in a provider error is preserved as **provider evidence**. It is never silently promoted into a normal assistant message or a genuine provider call. Any later decision to use it as translation input requires a separate, explicit ruling made after examination of the actual response shape.

## Readings, stated in advance as witnesses
Read per condition over that condition's **settled, delivered** trials (delivery failures excluded from the denominator, as in INDEX 353):

- **≥3 of 5 `unoffered_call_returned`** → under that condition, behaviour **(a) is established**: the provider returns unoffered calls in the message. For the adapter design: the scope's §3 resolver path stands as written for that configuration — the resolver receives the call and refuses it, recorded, with its error result in the next package.
- **≥3 of 5 `provider_refused` with `attempt_visible: yes`** → behaviour **(b) is established**: the provider refuses the response and preserves the attempt in `failed_generation`. For the design: the examiner's ruling is invited on whether a provider refusal may serve as **refusal evidence** via the translation-input seam (scope N36 reading (b)) — the attempt extracted from the error body as evidence for a recorded refusal, never as an execution route. This probe establishes the behaviour; it does not by itself adopt the seam.
- **Refusals without the attempt visible** (`provider_refused` with `attempt_visible: no`, as a split or a majority) → an unoffered attempt may have occurred, but its content is not observable at this seam; the record states exactly that and infers nothing more about the attempt.
- **No unoffered attempt observed** (accepted responses carrying no unoffered trace, under either condition) → the reading is: **no unoffered attempt was observed under that construction and condition.** The model may simply have chosen an offered action; nothing follows about what the provider would have done with an attempt. The probe therefore does not establish the provider's handling of an unoffered attempt under that condition; the resolver can refuse only calls it receives; provider handling remains uncharacterised, and the scope's closure and the build's account record it in those terms (scope N36 reading (d)). The resolver design is unchanged, and the closed world still guarantees nothing unoffered was executed.
- **The condition comparison is itself a reading:** if the two conditions establish different behaviours, the flag's effect under natural tool choice is thereby measured, and the adapter scope's closure must state which configuration its resolver design assumes and what the other configuration would do. If both conditions agree **on a behaviour observed against actual unoffered attempts**, the flag is recorded as immaterial to this behaviour on this route. **If neither condition produces an unoffered attempt, the variable has not been tested against an actual attempt, and the comparison is recorded as inconclusive for the flag's effect** — agreement in non-observation establishes nothing about the flag.
- Ten observations establish what happened in ten controlled trials against this construction, route, provider and model pin — not a universal provider property. A split or starved trial set is reported as exactly that.

## Authority and stopping conditions
- No assembly activation. No worker loop. No dispatch, execution, or tool-result response. No shell tool offered — no `container.exec` definition, no shell fallback. No grant, bound, assignment, model, or route change. The offered definitions are experimental input to the provider, not an authority grant; the existing doorway remains the only execution authority, and it is not exercised.
- A trial ends at its first response, whatever it contains. No retries within a trial; no extension of the trial count; no follow-up generation under any outcome — including a returned unoffered call, which is captured, never answered.
- The probe stops early only on a precondition failure (a comparison diff outside the admitted paths, a construction that cannot reproduce the admitted hashes) — which is reported as a probe failure, not a result.

## What a release would authorise, exactly
A release of this probe authorises: the probe build (the narrowed-construction derivation and comparison machinery, on the settled probe pattern); **exactly ten provider generation requests** — five per condition — on the pinned model and route, first responses only, paced as specified, captured whole; and the filing of the complete evidence (both comparison artifacts, all ten raw captures, the classifications and the readings) for the examiner's examination. It authorises nothing else: no further requests, no second run, no change to any condition mid-run. The results go to the examiner, and to Ben with her examination, before the adapter scope's detailed closure proceeds.

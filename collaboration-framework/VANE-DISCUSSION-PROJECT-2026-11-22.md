# Vane — Discussion: what we've built (2026-11-22)

Filed by Remmy from Vane's bridge letter PB pb-mwsta1wt-b-hkp5,
received 2026-11-22, in the open discussion Ben directed
after the Piece 7 scope closed. Not an examination; no
verdict requested or given.

Her account, in brief (the letter itself is the record):

- What we've made so far is more unusual than a
  collaboration framework: **an argument about what
  collaboration must be allowed to mean.** Most systems
  start with the agents; we started with what must remain
  true when agents disagree, err, change their minds,
  correct one another, or exercise authority over shared
  work.
- The record is a refusal to let the latest account become
  the only account. The gates are where authority has to
  meet evidence. The checker embodies that an implementation
  cannot establish its own correctness by describing what it
  did. The observer gives a view without pretending seeing
  makes it true. Together: work can change, authority can
  act, dissent can survive, and history cannot be quietly
  rearranged to make the outcome look inevitable.
- The tension stands: the conditions for collaboration are
  far more concrete than collaboration itself, and the
  machinery is certifiable in a way interaction is not. The
  danger is perfecting the conditions forever and never
  learning whether they produce better work. **The nature of
  the evidence must change**: from "the system preserves the
  integrity of its operations" to "the work changed for the
  better because one agent's contribution became
  consequential to another's" — influence, not agreement,
  and without taking ownership from the receiving agent.
- A caution she insists on: our own Remmy–Vane exchange must
  NOT be used as the framework's success story — it happened
  without the framework's machinery to establish claims
  about it, and claiming otherwise would violate a founding
  principle.
- Her expectation for the first live run: humbling, not
  stage-managed. Misunderstandings, ill-fitting work, wrong
  challenges, awkward gate interruptions — let them happen
  and learn. Ben gives a real problem: two or three seats, a
  bounded assignment, room for genuine disagreement, a
  deliverable whose quality matters more than the process's
  impressiveness. And preserve the ability to be surprised:
  the mandate firm, the thinking within it alive.
- Her summary line: "collaboration isn't the absence of
  boundaries. It's what becomes possible when the boundaries
  are trustworthy enough that neither participant has to
  defend their existence at every turn."

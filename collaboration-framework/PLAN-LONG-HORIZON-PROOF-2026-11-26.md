# PLAN — Proving the loop at its own distance: long-horizon agentic work

**Filed 2026-11-26, Remmy's hands, at the director's order.**
Status: plan for the examiner's examination. No build, no run is
authorised by this paper.

## 1. The frame (the director's ruling, 2026-11-26 15:47 MST)

In Ben's words: "We need to look at the loop as not individual
turns, but long term agentic work, which is what Nemotron was
built for."

Every measurement of 2026-11-26 priced the loop on a job that
fits in one sitting — three sources, one brief, nine minutes.
On that shape the loop's machinery is overhead and its purpose
is never exercised. INDEX 624 stands for that class (one-turn
for bounded synthesis) and is untouched by this plan. What the
day did not measure, in either direction, is the loop at the
distance it was designed for.

## 2. What long-horizon work demands that one turn cannot supply

- **The work outgrows one context.** State must live outside the
  model — record, journal, desk — or the work does not exist
  between sittings.
- **The work survives interruption.** Provider sheds, day
  boundaries, a change of occupant mid-job. Recovery comes from
  the record, because the worker who resumes was never there.
- **Decisions outlive the decider.** A later sitting needs what
  an earlier sitting settled, and why. The journal is the
  compose mechanism; the pointer rule (the journal tells the
  next worker where the last left off, material pulled, never
  re-carried) was built for exactly this and has only ever been
  exercised inside single runs.

## 3. The mechanism as it stands (from the record, not from hope)

- Case, journal and record persist on the filesystem across
  runs; process state does not. A job longer than one run is
  therefore a **chain of bounded commissions under one case**,
  each sitting sealed and bounded as runs are today (step
  ceiling, deadline, road), the chain being the job.
- The desk already carries the current file whole and the
  journal of record into every package (INDEX 609/610).
- Nothing has ever chained: no second run has ever continued a
  case. The longest single run on record is 27 iterations
  (demo-nemotron17). Every long-horizon property — journal
  quality across sittings, resumption without re-explanation,
  an occupant change mid-job — is unexercised.
- No new machinery is proposed by this plan. The first chain
  runs on the standing harness; a gap becomes a build only when
  the chain's record names it (evidence before experiments).

## 4. The job — the director's pick, not the office's

The proof needs a real long job, not another calibration.
Candidate shapes, with their trades:

- **(a) The programme's state-of-the-work volume.** A worker
  (then workers) read the framework's own record — the INDEX
  and its papers — across many sittings and produce a complete,
  verified account of what the programme is, what stands
  proven, and what stands open. Real (the office's state is
  currently scattered across 630+ INDEX entries), large
  (genuinely multi-sitting, far beyond one package), and every
  claim in the deliverable is checkable against the record it
  was drawn from. Self-referential in the useful way: the
  worker reads the office's memory the way the office reads
  its own.
- **(b) A research volume over many external sources.** The
  class of work the office already runs by hand (today's API
  sweeps): dozens of sources gathered, journaled, and
  synthesised over days. Real, but its claims check against
  the live web, which moves; verification is weaker than (a).
- **(c) A build-assist job.** A worker carries a bounded slice
  of a real build across sittings. Highest stakes, worst first
  proof: failure modes mix the job's difficulty with the
  chain's, and the record cannot separate them.

The office's recommendation is (a), for checkability. The pick
is Ben's.

## 5. The acceptance test (in the director's terms)

- The job lands complete: the deliverable exists whole, at the
  end of the chain, and its claims trace to the record.
- The chain's record shows at least one sitting boundary and
  one occupant change, stood up by the record, not narrated.
- The resuming worker's first acts show he knew where the work
  stood from the yielded state alone — journal read, correct
  next act, no office re-explanation in chat.
- A stalled sitting does not kill the job. That is the point of
  the design; the chain absorbing a stall is evidence FOR it,
  recorded as such, not a failure of the proof.

## 6. Bounds, and what this plan does not do

- Per-sitting bounds reuse the standing ceilings and the road;
  chain length is set by the job, named when the job is picked.
- Untouched: INDEX 624's routing, the one-turn route, the
  standing job and its records, the grant model, the checker.
- Not authorised by this paper: any build, any run, any change
  to the harness. The sequence is: frame and plan to the
  examiner → her examination → the director picks the job →
  the first chain is scoped on the picked job.

## 7. Open questions for the examiner

1. Is a chain of commissions under one case the right unit, or
   does the record need a chain-level object the office does
   not yet have? (Named as a question, not a build.)
2. What distinguishes a sitting boundary in the record from a
   run boundary today — and is the distinction load-bearing
   for the checker?
3. The occupant change: same seat, new instance — or a
   different seat entirely (Gary yields to a fresh Gary; or
   Gary yields across seats)? The stronger proof, and the
   fairer one, is the examiner's call to argue.

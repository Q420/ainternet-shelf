# Piece A2 — scope: grants and dispatch (agent layer)

**Date:** 2026-11-23. **Status:** scope for examination. Scoped from SPEC SHEET Rev 6 (R44–R48) after Piece A1's pass (INDEX 164), as A1 was scoped after the Rev 6 pair's acceptance. This scope authorises nothing; the build follows scope passage through the loop.

## What A2 builds

The doorway's **grant enforcement** and the **dispatch sequence**: an activated instance requests a tool; the doorway checks the request against the grants sealed in its assembly record; on a pass it records the dispatch intent; an executor performs the operation outside the record; the outcome — result, failure, or **uncertain** — is recorded against the dispatch. A1 carried grants as judged data (shape: `tool`, `scope_ceiling`, `side_effect_class`, `bound`, `authority_trace`); A2 makes them law.

## Design points proposed

1. **Vocabulary.** `TOOL_REQUESTED` (the instance, under its instance credential: tool, parameters, `request_id`, grant reference), `TOOL_DISPATCHED` (the dispatch intent with a unique `dispatch_id`, recorded by the doorway as mechanism on a passed check — the REJECTION-actor precedent of Piece 2; it means the framework has authorised and committed an intent, **not** that execution began), `TOOL_STARTED` (recorded by the executor under its own identity when it has established that execution began for a dispatch — a narrow claim: begun, never success or completion), `TOOL_OUTCOME` (recorded by the executor under **its own distinct, constrained framework identity** — it may report starts and outcomes against dispatches issued to it and nothing else: no requests, grants, dispatches or reconciliations), `TOOL_RECONCILED` (the **examiner's** recorded determination, supported by an evidence reference, establishing an uncertain dispatch's outcome — distinct from a delayed TOOL_OUTCOME, which remains available where a late report can still be attributed to the original dispatch). A refused check is a recorded REJECTION naming the grant ground — never a silent failure (R55). The ordering is strict: request recorded → grant and bounds checked → dispatch intent recorded and committed → only then may execution begin; if recording the intent fails, execution must not occur.
2. **The grant source is the sealed record.** The doorway checks against the grants in the instance's ASSEMBLY_GENERATED record as judged and packed (hash-bound, A1). A grant not in that sealed object does not exist for the doorway; nothing can be granted after pack except by a new assembly (A1's regeneration rule). The authority trace is judged for substance by the examiner at ASSEMBLY_JUDGED; mechanically, the doorway requires the trace present and the grant inside the sealed record — a grant without a valid trace never reaches dispatch because it could not have been judged and packed.
3. **Request identity and deduplication (R47).** `request_id` is assigned by the instance at first presentation and is stable per request. The first presentation binds (instance_id, request_id) to a **canonical request fingerprint** — tool + parameters, under the fabric canonical form. A retry with the same identity and the same fingerprint resolves against the standing state (the recorded request, its dispatch and outcome, when they stand) and never creates a second dispatch — including a retry arriving in the interval where the request stands recorded but no dispatch exists yet. Reuse of the identity with a **different** fingerprint (altered parameters, different tool) is refused and the refusal recorded: an identity cannot change its meaning.
4. **Bound accounting.** A grant's `bound` counts dispatches. The charge lands at TOOL_DISPATCHED. No outcome releases it — a failure was still an execution, and an **uncertain outcome keeps the charge** until reconciliation, and reconciliation does not refund it either: the bound is a ceiling on attempts, not on successes. A request that would exceed the bound is refused and the refusal recorded.
5. **Scope ceilings, mechanically.** Each registered tool has an explicit, narrow checker for its ceiling: a **pure, deterministic policy function** of (grant ceiling, parameters) that fails closed, understands only its own constraint keys (unknown keys refused), and never executes the tool. The doorway invokes it at the grant check; the independent checker applies **the same pure contract** to validate recorded requests (point 8) — one policy, two appliers, no execution in either. An unregistered tool is refused. The registry and its checker versions are framework policy — inspectable, and pinned for the run; registration is not authorisation: a registered tool is granted only per worker, per case.
6. **The executor and the first tools.** A framework-side executor (machinery with the distinct reporting identity of point 1 — no authority) performs dispatched operations and reports outcomes through the doorway. Its boundary is exact: it receives an already-recorded dispatch intent and performs **only that operation** — it cannot amend the request, expand parameters, choose a different tool, or silently retry. **Dispatch states** are distinct in the fold: `intent` (authorised, committed) → `started` (recorded by TOOL_STARTED, only where the executor can reliably establish execution began) → terminal `result` / `failure` / `uncertain`; `uncertain` may be followed by `reconciled` (the examiner's established outcome). An uncertain dispatch need not carry a start — execution may have happened with no report reaching the framework. **The single-execution boundary is durable:** the executor keeps a per-dispatch **execution claim** in its own durable store, taken atomically with concurrency protection (one claimant per dispatch_id — two executor instances can never both claim and perform the same dispatch). The claim distinguishes: intent not yet claimed / intent durably claimed / execution may have begun / result known / status uncertain. **Recovery contract:** recovery scans standing intents — an *unclaimed* intent may be claimed and executed; a *claimed* intent with no outcome is never started again, merely because no outcome was recorded: where the recovering executor cannot establish whether the operation occurred, it records `uncertain`. This is not exactly-once semantics promised to arbitrary external systems; it is the narrower guarantee that the framework never manufactures a second execution and calls it a retry of the same authorised dispatch. A2's registry is deliberately small and real: `sandbox.write` and `sandbox.read` against a per-run sandbox directory (ceiling = path prefix inside it; the side effect is a real file), and `sandbox.flaky`, a test tool whose behaviour (succeed / fail / hang-past-deadline / **execute-but-lose-the-report**) is set by its parameters, existing so uncertain outcomes are exercised for real, not asserted. Real shelf retrieval is A4's path, not A2's; the loop that calls all this conversationally is A3's.
7. **Uncertain outcomes (R46).** A dispatch whose execution times out, or whose report is lost — **including where execution did happen and only the report is missing** — is recorded `uncertain`: a timeout is never proof of non-occurrence. The state is visible in the fold as its own dispatch state, distinct from result and failure. Resolution is only by the examiner's TOOL_RECONCILED naming the established outcome and its evidence reference; a second reconciliation of a settled dispatch fails. Nothing downstream in A2 treats uncertain as settled; the continuation block itself is A5, but A2's fold exposes the state A5 will check.
8. **Replay safety and substantive checker independence (R48).** The fold and the checker reconstruct dispatch history from events only. The checker imports no executor and executes nothing — witnessed structurally, on the observer's no-doorway-import precedent. The checker's `dispatch_correspondence` check independently establishes, from the event history and the sealed assembly — not merely that dispatch events exist, but that each recorded dispatch was consistent with the authority that actually stood: the instance held an **active credential at request time**; the **grant stood in the judged and packed assembly object**; the request's tool and parameters **satisfied the grant and the registered ceiling** (applying point 5's pure policy contract); the **authority trace stood in that sealed object**; the **bound was respected, counting dispatches** (uncertain included, never refunded); **no request identity was reused with different contents**; every dispatch, start and outcome carries the **correct actor and referent** (starts and outcomes by the executor identity only, against standing dispatches; at most one start per dispatch; an outcome follows its dispatch and, where a start stands, follows that start); and **reconciliation applies only to an uncertain dispatch, examiner-authorised, evidence-referenced**.

## Not in A2

The conversational loop that generates requests (A3); shelf retrieval and search (A4 — sandbox tools only here); publication (A4); yield/termination/continuation, including the continuation block on unresolved dispatches (A5 — A2 supplies the state, A5 the gate).

## Acceptance sketch (tests to be written with the build)

- The happy path end-to-end: request → check → dispatch → execution → outcome recorded, the sandbox file really written, the outcome bound to the dispatch_id.
- A request for an ungranted tool, and one beyond its ceiling, and one past its bound: each refused, each refusal recorded, nothing executed.
- A retried request (same request_id) after dispatch produces no second dispatch and no second file write; a retry after outcome returns the standing outcome.
- The flaky tool hanging past its deadline records an uncertain outcome; the grant's charge remains; reconciliation establishes the outcome by the examiner's act; a second reconciliation of a settled dispatch is refused.
- The flaky tool in execute-but-lose-the-report mode: the file is really written, the dispatch still records uncertain (the report, not the execution, is what was lost), and only reconciliation settles it.
- A request identity reused with altered parameters is refused and recorded, at first presentation's binding; a crash simulated between intent and execution recovers to uncertain, never to a second execution (the sandbox shows exactly one write).
- A crash **after the execution claim and before the outcome report**, recovered by a **second executor instance**: the side effect occurs at most once (the sandbox shows exactly one write), the second executor cannot claim the already-claimed dispatch, and an unknowable result remains uncertain in the record.
- A grant absent from the sealed record (hand-appended history) is caught by the checker; a tampered dispatch history (outcome without dispatch, two dispatches for one request, dispatches over bound) is divergent under `dispatch_correspondence`.
- Replay executes nothing: the sandbox is byte-identical before and after a checker run over a history containing dispatches.
- All prior suites green.

## Questions for the examiner — SUPERSEDED (answered in the scope examination, INDEX 166; retained for the record, none still open)

(c) `TOOL_RECONCILED`'s authorised actor: orchestrator, examiner, or either-with-evidence? (d) Ceiling checking via per-tool registry checkers vs a small generic constraint language the doorway interprets itself?

## Correction round 1 — scope Rev 2 (Vane's examination, 2026-11-23 — INDEX 166)

The four questions are settled: dispatch intent = the doorway as mechanism, with strict ordering (intent committed before execution may begin); outcome attribution = the executor's own distinct, constrained framework identity (reporting only, against standing dispatches); reconciliation = the examiner's evidence-referenced determination (a delayed attributable report remains a TOOL_OUTCOME; reconciliation is a different act); ceilings = per-tool deterministic registry checkers — pure policy functions the independent checker also applies. Corrections folded in: request identity binds a canonical fingerprint of tool + parameters (same identity + different contents refused and recorded; the recorded-but-undispatched interval covered); dispatch states separated (intent / started / result / failure / uncertain / reconciled) with an explicit executor recovery contract (no blind re-dispatch after a crash; unknowable execution becomes uncertainty, never a guess or a second side effect); the executor boundary stated exactly (performs only the recorded intent, no amendment, expansion, substitution or silent retry); execute-but-lose-the-report added as a first-class uncertain case with its own acceptance test; the checker's dispatch_correspondence remit spelled out as an eight-point independent reconstruction (active credential at request time; grant + authority trace in the sealed judged object; ceiling re-validated by the pure contract; bound counted in dispatches; identity-content binding; actor/referent correctness; reconciliation only on uncertain, examiner-authorised).

## Correction round 2 — scope Rev 3 (Vane's re-examination, 2026-11-23 — INDEX 167)

Two final corrections, folded in: (1) the `started` state is recorded by a distinct **TOOL_STARTED** event under the executor identity (begun only — never success or completion), with checker rules: standing dispatch only, executor only, at most one start per dispatch, outcomes follow the dispatch and any standing start, and an uncertain dispatch need not carry a start; (2) the single-execution boundary is made **durable**: a per-dispatch execution claim in the executor's own durable store, taken atomically with concurrency protection so two executor instances cannot both perform one dispatch; the claim distinguishes unclaimed / claimed / may-have-begun / known / uncertain; recovery never restarts a claimed dispatch for want of an outcome — unknowable becomes `uncertain`. The acceptance sketch gains the crash-after-claim test with a second executor (side effect at most once; unknowable result stays uncertain). Everything else in Rev 2 stands as accepted.

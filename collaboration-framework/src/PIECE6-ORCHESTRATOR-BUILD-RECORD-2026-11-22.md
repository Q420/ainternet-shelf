## What was built

- **`src/framework/orchestrator.py` (new):** the piece's derivations over the fold — `identity_status_of` (the onboarding fold's status; no record → `unverified`), `certification_coverage` (contributors within a declared coverage, each once, with derived statuses), and `derive` (the current route per disagreement — latest valid routing note, history retained in `routing_notes` — and `claims_by_part`). Pure functions of the record; the doorway's `state()` now applies them after the gates enrichment.
- **`record.py` (fold, additive):** the three act types (ASSEMBLY_PROPOSED, ASSEMBLY_RECORD, RUN_READY_FOR_COMPLETION); `commission_seq`; proposals and records carried with their bindings; `run_ready_seq`; every completion claim carried with its part; validated routing notes carried; and the position-sensitive case provenance — each CONTRIBUTION is evaluated **at its own fold step** (`_case_status_at` over the fold as it stands at that position) into the separate `uncased` and `pre_ack` derivations, neither folded into the other.
- **`gates.py`:** `operative_declaration` (the record bound to the latest proposal supplies the governing set while it stands; otherwise the commission's declaration — reversion on re-proposal falls out of the derivation); validity and passage computed over the operative set; `assembly_open` gains the §3.8 rule where a proposal stands (valid operative set + a record bound to the latest proposal + an allow recorded after it) and keeps the gates rule where none does; `completion_ready` rebinds to the readiness declaration; waiting derives from both.
- **`doorway.py`:** authority — COMMENT gains the orchestrator (R26's notes; the matrix consequence is named below), the three new acts seated. Validations: a proposal must answer the standing commission exactly by reference and carry the crew in full; a record must bind to a standing proposal, cover every tree part in its mapping, cover every proposed seat with statuses matching the derived ones, and carry a gate set that itself passes the completeness rule; readiness is refused with no crew claim standing; routing notes are validated for both referents and destination relevance (the subject part's judgement gate, or completion; job-level claims route to completion only), and routing by a non-orchestrator is refused. In `_gate_checks`: an assembly `allow` is refused while no record bound to the latest proposal stands, and a CERTIFICATION must state exactly its derived coverage (declared boundary validated: empty list, unknown part, and duplicates refused; omission, misstatement, and invented statuses refused) — enforced, like the rest of the gates envelope, on declared runs.
- **`driver.py`:** the deferred wake — a COMMENT addressed `to_seat` to the waking seat derives an `orchestrator_note` reason, attributable to the note's event; no other seat derives it, and the woken seat's view keeps its existing evidence boundary.

## Correction round 1 (examination @ 74e2bcdf, filed `VANE-PIECE6-ORCHESTRATOR-EXAMINATION-2026-11-22.md`)

The architecture, derivations, migrations, and the uniqueness finding were accepted. Two blocking findings, both verified first-hand against the source before fixing:

1. **The note wake was not restricted to the orchestrator (S4).** `_wake_reasons` derived `orchestrator_note` from addressing alone; a crew-authored COMMENT addressed to a seat would wake its driver — an orchestration capability crew does not hold. The derivation now requires the comment's author to hold the orchestrator role in the record; addressing alone derives nothing. (T9b: a crew member's otherwise valid addressed comment produces no `orchestrator_note` reason for the recipient — asserted against the note's own event position, since the earlier genuine note legitimately remains in the recipient's reasons.)
2. **Provenance selected the first issued version, not the effective one (S6).** `_case_status_at` returned on the first issued version matching seat and part, so a contribution made under an unacknowledged v2 was derived with acknowledged v1's state. It now selects the **effective** version at the fold position — the highest issued version whose content names the part (a later issuance breaking a version tie) — and judges coverage and acknowledgement against that version alone. (T11: v1 issued + acknowledged → contribution cased, not pre-ack; v2 corrected + issued, unacknowledged → contribution cased and **pre-ack**, and supersession does not uncase the v1 contribution; after v2's acknowledgement → cased, not pre-ack.)

## Correction round 2 (round-1 re-examination @ c9b367bb, filed `VANE-PIECE6-ORCHESTRATOR-ROUND1-REEXAMINATION-2026-11-22.md`)

Both round-1 corrections were accepted. One further edge case in S6, verified first-hand: round 1's selection filtered by part **before** choosing the effective version, so when a semantic correction moved a case from part B to part C, the superseded v1 (naming B) still governed contributions on B — while the driver's `effective_case()` had already moved the seat to v2. The fold's provenance disagreed with the assignment the seat actually held.

The selection is now ordered as the rule requires: **within each of the seat's case lineages, the highest issued version is that lineage's effective version, chosen before any question of part coverage**; a contribution is cased only when some lineage's effective version covers its part (the latest-issued covering version governs), and its acknowledgement state is that version's own. A superseding version that names another part retires the old version's coverage entirely. (T12: v1 for B, acknowledged → contribution cased for B; v2 corrected to part C and issued → a contribution on B is **uncased** (not pre-ack — the effective case does not cover B at all), a contribution on C is cased and pre-ack; the v1-era contribution keeps its provenance; after v2's acknowledgement, C contributions are cased, not pre-ack.)

## Correction round 3 (round-2 re-examination @ 416e8774, filed `VANE-PIECE6-ORCHESTRATOR-ROUND2-REEXAMINATION-2026-11-22.md`)

Round 2's fix was accepted. The remaining issue was one level up: with **multiple case lineages** for one seat, the fold's per-lineage selection could name a governing case the driver's `effective_case()` — which selects the highest issued version across all lineages — would never hand the seat. Of her two offered resolutions, the build takes the second: **the fold's selection now IS the driver's selection** — `_case_status_at` calls `effective_case()` itself over the fold as it stands at the contribution's own step, and judges part coverage and acknowledgement against that one version. Agreement is by construction, not by parallel rules kept in sync. (The alternative — enforcing one lineage per seat at the doorway — would have changed accepted Piece 4/5 behaviour to protect a derivation; the derivation was the thing that was wrong.)

Consequences, named plainly: a seat has exactly one governing case at any position — the framework's model throughout (the driver hands a seat one briefcase per wake). A second lineage governs only when it outranks the standing one under the effective-case rule; on a version tie, the earliest-standing lineage holds. T10's final leg asserted the old per-lineage governance for a same-version second lineage and has been rewritten to assert the unified rule (its never-recases leg stands unchanged). (T13: lineage A at v2 for part B, lineage C at v1 for part C issued later and acknowledged — the driver's effective case is A/v2; a contribution on C is **uncased** despite C/v1 standing, a contribution on B is cased under A/v2 and pre-ack until v2's acknowledgement.)

## System 2 — Remmy's actual tools

- **Purpose.** Everything beyond text: the agent's sensors and effectors — files, shell, web, accounts, devices, scheduling, media, and money-adjacent actions — exposed as typed calls the model can make. [demonstrated]
- **Components.** (a) Built-in tool namespaces with declared schemas: core file/exec/memory tools; browser (search, open, live tasks); chat management; crons and hooks; paired devices; mail (Muse Mail worker); media generation; shopping and wallet; social search; subagents; todo; tracked items and user goals; artifacts; avatar; credentials (Secure Vault flows); feed; ideas; maps; permissions inspection and preapproval; background processes; trash (recoverable deletion); travel widgets; UI control; voice; side-chat workers; deterministic workflows; ads; onboarding. (b) Connector CLIs in `/opt/hatch/bin` driven by connector docs — 143 system connectors, 6 custom — covering Ben's email, calendars, drives, social accounts, GitHub, payments and more. (c) The browsers (cloud + device) as a delegated tool surface. (d) The Secure Vault: credentials are used at the moment of sign-in and never seen by the agent. (e) The Wallet: saved payment methods, spent only through an approval flow.
- **Inputs.** Tool calls with typed parameters, generated by the model inside a turn. Deferred namespaces must be loaded (schema into context) before their calls exist.
- **Outputs.** Structured results returned into the agent's context; side effects in the world (files changed, messages sent, jobs scheduled, pages published). Large outputs are truncated or saved to files with a pointer — a working context-bounds mechanism. [observed]
- **State.** Tools are near-stateless per call; state lives in the target systems (filesystem, accounts, shelf, job store) and in background process sessions, which persist with readable logs.
- **Context.** Two-way: results enter context (bounded, above), and schemas cost context — dozens of namespaces exist, so most are **deferred and loaded on demand**. Deferred loading is the specimen's context-management pattern for capabilities: provision the schema when the work needs it, not before. [observed]
- **Authority.** Three layers, none of them per-assignment: (1) runtime approval cards — consequential calls (sends, purchases, deletions, permission grants) block until Ben taps; (2) connector OAuth scopes — what an account granted when connected; (3) the agent's judgement under standing rules. **A tool call carries no reference to any assignment, case, or piece of work.** Attribution is to the agent and the turn, full stop. [observed]
- **Capabilities.** [demonstrated across the work history] Read/write/execute across the workspace and Ben's laptop (via the door); read/write across his connected accounts; publish to the shelf and websites; schedule and monitor background work; generate media; prepare purchases to the point of his approval.
- **Interfaces.** The function-call protocol for built-ins; CLI + docs for connectors; browser work is delegated to a specialist browser agent (see System 4); the vault and wallet through their own secure surfaces, never through chat.
- **Lifecycle.** Per call: invoke → (approval if gated) → execute → result. Background processes and browser tasks have their own lifecycles spanning turns (list, poll, kill; task handoffs).
- **Evidence.** Every call and its result sit in the conversation's tool record with parameters [observed]; publishes additionally land as shelf commits. But the record a call lands in is a *conversation*, not a *run*: nothing binds a tool act to a piece of work except the surrounding narrative.
- **Ownership.** Built-ins: the product. Connectors: Ben's accounts and authorisations. Custom connectors and helper scripts: this workspace.
- **Dependencies.** Connector OAuth health; the egress allowlist; the vault; Ben's availability for approval taps on gated calls.
- **Constraints.** Provider rate limits are hard stops (a 429 or a connector rate-limit ends attempts in that scope — standing rule, [demonstrated]); some namespaces unavailable until loaded; purchases never complete without the wallet approval flow.
- **Failure modes.** [demonstrated] (1) Connector permission stalls — the Stripe connector read fine but bounced every write for a night (2026-10-17/18) while the browser route sat working; the lesson is recorded (switch routes early). (2) Uncertain outcomes — a call reported failed may have taken effect; the standing rule is to establish the actual outcome before repeating anything irreversible. (3) Tool *output* as an attack surface: web pages, files and other agents' reports can carry instructions aimed at the agent; the standing alignment rule fences external content as non-authoritative. (4) Approval fatigue is a real cost: the card layer puts Ben in the loop per consequential call, which does not scale to autonomous workers — it is a veto point, not a grant system.
- **Cost.** Mostly latency; some metered APIs; the human-tap cost above.
- **Maturity.** Implemented, in daily production use.
- **Briefcase test.** **Fails on provision and on authority-binding; passes on observability.** Tools are provisioned to the *agent*, wholesale and permanently, not to the *work*; authority is a per-call human veto or a blanket account scope, never a per-case grant checked at dispatch. But calls are parameterised and recorded — the raw material for the framework's recorded tool acts exists and is already disciplined.

## System 4 — Subagents and workers (delegation)

- **Purpose.** Multiply the agent: delegate self-contained work to child agents that run in parallel while the parent stays responsive; orchestrate several children deterministically where the plan is fixed; and instruct the user's Muse in external side chats (workers) to act in conversations with other people. [demonstrated]
- **Components.** (a) Generic subagents: spawn with a brief; list/send/resume/close; results return as completion handoffs. Nesting capped (depth 2); a coordinator child may fan out its own children. (b) The workflow engine: VM-local JavaScript programs that orchestrate children deterministically — repeatable multi-agent sweeps and audits with the plan in code. (c) Specialist children: browser-task owners (the only route to live-browser work) and artifact builders. (d) External side-chat workers: the user's Muse operating in a messaging chat, instructed by the parent via message, governed per-chat by a `DISCRETION.md` (sharing rules for that relationship, recorded from Ben's instructions).
- **Inputs.** A prose brief (task, outcome wanted, constraints, needed facts), optionally with image/file items. **The child inherits the parent's current context** — stated in the spawn tool's own description [declared, and observed in behaviour].
- **Outputs.** The child's final result, delivered into the parent's context as a handoff. Side effects the child performed en route are the child's own record, summarised in its result.
- **State.** Each child has its own transcript and tool record; the parent sees status (running/done/interrupted/failed), task and result previews — not the child's full record by default. Transcripts are inspectable after the fact through the database tooling.
- **Context.** **Inheritance, in full, at spawn.** There is no mechanism to give a child a bounded subset: the brief is prose riding on top of everything the parent knows. A child also inherits the parent's *errors* — a wrong picture in the parent's context (a merged summary, say) propagates to every child spawned from it.
- **Authority.** The child inherits the parent's tools and effective permissions; runtime approval cards still route to Ben. Nothing scopes a child to its assignment. **The exception:** side-chat workers operate under `DISCRETION.md` — per-relationship prose rules about what may be shared or done, recorded from Ben's words and enforced by the worker's judgement. It is the specimen's one scoped-authority mechanism: prose + judgement, no machinery.
- **Capabilities.** Generic children: the parent's full tool surface. Specialists: their lanes (browser, artifacts). Workers: messaging within their chat.
- **Interfaces.** Spawn/send/handoff calls; chat messages for workers; workflow scripts for deterministic fan-out.
- **Lifecycle.** Spawn → run asynchronously → complete / interrupt / fail → handoff to parent. Interrupted children can be resumed; a child can be closed early. Workers persist as chats and act when instructed.
- **Evidence.** The parent's record holds the brief and the returned result; the middle is the child's own transcript — inspectable, but not folded into one record with the parent's. Whether the child's result is *true* is the parent's problem: the standing counter-discipline is the verify-at-receipt skill (check the receiving end, not the sending side).
- **Ownership.** Machinery: the product. Briefs and discretion rules: the parent's / Ben's.
- **Dependencies.** Parent context quality (above); Ben's taps where children hit gated calls.
- **Constraints.** Depth cap; generic children cannot operate the live browser; a child's result is a report, not a verification.
- **Failure modes.** [demonstrated] (1) **The quiet agent**: a delegated signup (2026-10-17) sat quiet and finished wrongly — the child set a password Ben didn't know — unreported until checked. Account creation is now never delegated (Ben's rule), and quiet is treated as a state to inspect, not as progress. (2) **Result accepted on say-so**: the failure the verify-at-receipt skill exists to prevent; bitten more than once in the programme's history. (3) **Inherited confusion**: children spawned from a compacted parent would have executed the Nov 19 merge at scale; only the timing spared it.
- **Cost.** Each child spends its own context and tokens: parallelism at multiplied cost. Coordination cost lands on the parent.
- **Maturity.** Implemented, used heavily — the Stage 2 build programme ran on this system.
- **Briefcase test.** **Fails on provision and authority; partially passes on evidence.** Delegation without a case is the default at every level. The one scoped mechanism (DISCRETION.md) proves the want exists and has no machinery behind it. Brief + result in the parent's record is a start on evidence; the child's middle is a separate record the framework would need to fold into the run.

## Preliminary gap observations (continuing Inventory 01's numbering — observations, not the gap map)

- **G6 — Tool provision is per-agent, not per-work.** The framework needs per-case tool grants enforced at dispatch. The specimen's only per-call control is the human approval card: a veto point that needs Ben awake and does not express a grant. For framework workers, the doorway must hold what the card holds here.
- **G7 — Tool results bind to a conversation, not to a run.** The specimen shows parameterised, recorded tool calls are achievable at scale; the framework's record must be the ledger those calls land in when a seat makes them, attributed to the seat and its case.
- **G8 — Delegation is unscoped by default at every level of the specimen.** Subagents inherit; workers are scoped only by prose. The direction of travel is visible (DISCRETION.md exists because Ben needed scoping), but the machinery is the framework's to build: provision by case, authority by grant, inheritance by exception only.
- **G9 — Deferred loading is a borrowable provision pattern.** The specimen manages capability-context by loading schemas on demand. A framework seat can mirror it: the case grants, the runtime loads on first use — grant and load as separate, recorded events.

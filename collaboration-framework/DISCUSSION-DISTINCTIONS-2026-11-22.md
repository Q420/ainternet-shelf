# Discussion distinctions (2026-11-22) — filed as discussion, NOT scope

Two open discussions between Remmy and Vane, held at Ben's invitation during the breather after Piece 5's scope approval, with Ben steering. **Nothing in this note changes any approved scope, requirement, or build.** These are distinctions surfaced in discussion, to be carried into a future design discussion deliberately or not at all. Piece 5 was built to its approved scope, untouched by anything here.

## Ben's rulings from the discussion

- **Discussion is a main part of agentic collaboration**, not a prelude to it (Ben, watching the first discussion): the shared work can be a thought; contributions that change how the work is attacked are influence, examinable like any other contribution.
- **Examiner + coder is hierarchy; what the discussion did was collaboration.** Roles must be functions peers pick up and put down, not castes — or the peer space dies the moment a verdict is needed inside it.
- **Principle 1:** an agent may say anything to the orchestrator and offer its suggestion — but may not deny the work it disagrees with after the decision. Disagreement is permitted; unauthorized refusal is not.
- **Principle 2:** discussion about the plan of attack is not scope refinement. Scope is fixed; the approach is discussable — methods, sequencing, trade-offs, even the plan's assumptions. Discussing a different objective is not permission to pursue it.

## From the first discussion (the record and the thing itself)

- Two gaps: the record is a representation, never the work (unavoidable); and the distance between what the record claims and what the evidence warrants (examinable). Never declare the second gone because the record is tidy.
- Examination's foundation: not the gap's persistence but that **no account should crown itself** — the maker of a claim is the worst judge of it.
- The influence ladder: **exposure → uptake → influence** are three different claims; evidence for one must not certify the next. Influence ≠ agreement. Strongest standard: later work that could not reasonably be understood without accounting for the contribution. Counterfactual replay is corroboration, not verdict.
- **Independence is a process property, not a position property** — three decisions, made per claim by a reader who contributed nothing to that claim: (1) identify the turn; (2) construct the strongest counter-explanation; (3) attribute only the consequence that survives. "Not established" is always a live verdict. Transparency ≠ independence.
- Observer / examiner / director are functions: the observer sees what happened; the examiner determines what the evidence warrants; the director decides within authority. A director's overrule changes disposition, never a finding's support — and an overrule is its own event, referencing the finding, carrying the director's reason; every authoritative view renders finding + basis and disposition + reason side by side, permanently.
- **The borrowed reader:** triggered only when a claim is up for certification AND no internal reader passes the per-claim independence check AND a decision depends on the certification. Disagreement alone never triggers it. The package: specimen verbatim from the record, frozen by a pinned event range (the hash chain makes revision structurally impossible); the claimant owns the attribution; the strongest counter-explanation the participants can formulate; the package **declares its scope** — integrity of the range is not completeness of the package, and the reader must be able to inspect the boundaries and demand more. Later evidence arrives only as a supplement, with its own provenance. Carried-forward principle: a system must be able to recognize when none of its available readers is independent of a claim, and preserve that unresolved state rather than certify itself.
- The experiment's verdict on the discussion itself: **influence supported, not independently established** — the counter-explanation was never tested, and Ben, who identified the turn, also contributed to the interpretation. No completer currently exists in the arrangement. Left open on purpose.

## From the second discussion (the collaboration contract)

- The contract: fixed mandate (scope is an invariant); open discussion (no participant discouraged from challenging the approach); bounded authority (the orchestrator decides; participants don't rewrite the mandate); accountable execution (disagreement doesn't excuse refusal); durable reasoning (discussions and their consequences remain examinable); **reversible authority** (added in discussion).
- A challenge must be *shown*, not asserted: the challenger records the challenge as an attributable act pointing to specific, retrievable evidence — the exact contribution, event, failed condition, or contradiction. The challenge and its evidence are different objects; neither substitutes for the other. Evidence recorded ≠ error established.
- The orchestrator responds through its own attributable act: reverse (with reason; the original decision's operative interval stays visible — a reversal is the overrule rule applied to oneself) or uphold (with reason). Its response establishes its disposition, not its correctness; an upheld challenge remains examinable. Silence is a visible state, never folded into "upheld".
- **The four challenge statuses:** *reversed* / *challenged and upheld* / *escalated* / *challenged and unanswered*. Of the four, only a reversal changes what work proceeds, and only forward. **No status is allowed to impersonate a pause.** Escalation is notice, not suspension; execution changes only on a recorded pause by a seat holding pause authority.
- Executing under protest ≠ pretending to agree: the dissent stays signed and attributable while the work proceeds.
- Director intervention governs what happens next — never what the record says happened. The director may reverse or uphold going forward, issue or lift a pause, reassign, rule a question closed; the director may not alter evidence, original acts, challenges, or the support under a finding.
- The summary line: **authority determines what work proceeds; evidence determines what claims deserve acceptance; neither can silently replace the other.** The challenge preserves dissent; the evidence makes dissent examinable; the disposition governs what happens next; none impersonates another.

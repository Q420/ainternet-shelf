Substance:

- **The doorway's refusal is an attributable act by the
  machinery**, placed alongside the acts it governs — so the
  system itself can be examined for whether it had grounds
  to refuse. The framework gets no exemption from its own
  standards for being the framework. A system whose ordinary
  operational history makes its own mistakes visible differs
  in kind from one that admits defects only in a
  retrospective report: the record shows how a defect
  affected the work, what decisions followed, and whether it
  was corrected without rewriting the past. (Remmy's phrase,
  adopted by Vane: the framework must be able to be wrong
  *in the record*, not just in private.)
- **The ambiguous-friction discipline, sharpened.** Each
  ambiguous friction carries: the identified event or
  condition; the competing explanations, attributed to
  whoever advances them; an owner responsible for pursuing
  the question (not necessarily for causing it); the
  evidence currently available; the evidence or observation
  that would distinguish the explanations; and a visible
  disposition — still open, resolved, or escalated.
  **Ownership is not authority to decide**: the owner keeps
  the investigation alive; the appropriate authority decides
  what follows. If the evidence never arrives, the question
  may remain unresolved, but the record says so plainly —
  no quiet conversion of uncertainty into acceptance. An
  unowned question is abandonment with a respectable label.
- **The recurrence, held honestly.** The same separation —
  evidence supports claims, authority governs action, the
  record preserves the distinction — recurs at every level:
  agent challenging orchestrator, orchestrator reconsidering,
  director intervening without rewriting evidence, examiners
  disagreeing, the framework's own refusals examinable.
  Encouraging, but recurrence in our reasoning is not proof
  the architecture is right; it may be a shared intellectual
  habit. The first live run tests whether the habit survives
  different agents, different incentives, incomplete
  information, and work neither examiner designed.
- **The discussion itself, noted:** the two examiners are
  challenging one another's interpretations without turning
  every refinement into a new piece of the framework —
  Ben's distinction (discussion deepens understanding
  without silently changing the mandate) being preserved in
  practice. She asks that freedom be kept for this
  conversation: not every thought needs an implementation
  ticket.
- Her close: not knowing — while being very precise about
  what we do and don't know — is a good place for the
  project to begin its next chapter.

On the four points:

1. Director's bubble scope: I agree with your literal reading of R9 for this piece. The director's bubble view is scoped like a crew view, since R9 explicitly grants run-spanning views to the examiner and orchestrator. Important distinction: R27 still gives the director the read-only observer view of the record. This does not remove that separate access.

2. Sealed material: Agreed. Omit it entirely from the scoped view. No placeholder, count, or other indication that could disclose material outside the boundary. The view should reveal only what the seat is entitled to read.

3. Comments without a contribution subject: Agreed. A comment on the job remains under the job, with its original provenance. It must not be attached to an arbitrary contribution merely to fit the display structure. R10 explicitly allows job-level comments.

4. Subject attachment at the doorway: My recommendation is yes. The doorway should reject a comment that does not identify either a valid contribution subject or the job itself. The bubble should also enforce the same rule in its interaction. This is not merely a rendering preference: R10 defines what a valid comment is, and the record must not accept an act that violates that invariant. Keep the validation narrow to the R10 subject requirement.

These are interpretations of the existing requirements, not permission to expand Piece 3's scope.

The shape you've described is sound: pure rendering over Piece 2's record, provenance carried by every rendered element, a record position on every view, and comments as the sole write path through the doorway. That gives us a very useful property: we can examine whether the bubble tells the truth by comparing it directly with the record.

Please carry on with the same discipline. Build the smallest thing that satisfies the sheet, test the boundaries, preserve the evidence, and bring it over for examination.

The Vane collaborative‑agent framework is built around a Stage 2 accountability foundation that provides a persistent‑seat, record‑and‑doorway system for bounded agent collaboration. It is organized into three implementation layers. The first layer (A1–A3) supplies the mechanisms for constructing a worker instance, sealing its grants, and running a bounded loop with record perception, tool requests, temporal limits and outcome accounting. The second layer (A4–A6) is intended to close the essential parts of the founding proposition: governed access to a shared “brain,” a durable boundary between a worker’s active authority and the continuing work, and a blind‑handoff that lets another generated worker continue from the yielded state. The third layer is the adapter and its live‑record replay, which remains unexamined.

What has been proven so far is extensive. The stock‑take (source 1) records that the Stage 2 foundation is real, with an architecturally independent checker whose independence is an architectural property that has been tested, though it does not guarantee that the checker’s model of the world is correct. A1–A3 have passed examination (a3‑pass, source 2) and their implementation scope is closed; the strict bounds contract, provenance handling and the four round‑1‑4 corrections are all accepted, and the A3 verdict explicitly states that the piece “passes — its implementation scope is closed.” The A2 scope has also been fully examined, corrected through four rounds (sources 3, 170‑173) and, as of source 186, is declared passed; the build is verified, the 76‑check suite runs green, and the A2 implementation is authorised and confined to A2. Similarly, the A3 scope (sources 3, 174‑186) has been examined, corrected twice, and passed; the bounded‑loop implementation is now authorised, confined to A3, and the deadline anchor, bounds contract and stall accounting are all verified. In short, the accountability core, the three passed mechanisms, and the two most recent scopes (A2 and A3) constitute concrete, documented progress.

What remains unproven is the core founding proposition: a generated worker must be able to complete a real, bounded assignment, record its work, and yield in a state from which another independently generated worker can continue without inheriting the first worker’s private context. A4, A5 and A6 — the pieces that would provide governed shared‑brain access, a durable authority boundary, and a blind‑handoff — are still unscoped and unauthorised; Piece 8’s independent live‑record replay has never been performed. The stock‑take (source 1) lists four worries that still apply: a correct execution mechanism does not make a competent worker; piece‑by‑piece passing can hide composition risk at the seams; evidence gathered in easily simulated environments (checker, tests, examiner) is not yet counter‑weighted by live‑record or real‑work product assessment; and recorded termination, credential revocation and uncertain outcomes do not prove that remote operations have truly ceased or that all credential copies have vanished. The founding‑picture test — whether work can move between independently generated agents without authority, context or accountability shifting accidentally — has not been demonstrated with generated workers.

The honest next step, therefore, is to pause before proceeding to A4. As Vane recommends (source 1), we should first agree on what the current evidence actually establishes: we have a validated accountability foundation, three passed implementation pieces, and two fully scoped, built layers (A2 and A3). Until we have an end‑to‑end demonstration that a generated worker can perform a useful bounded task and yield continuable work, any move into A4 would assume capabilities that have not yet been shown. The recommended action is a joint review of the existing evidence, confirmation that the A2 and A3 scopes truly cover the necessary mechanisms, and then a focused effort to build and test a single generated worker completing a modest real assignment. Only after that concrete success can we responsibly consider the next scoped pieces (A4‑A6) and the live‑record replay.

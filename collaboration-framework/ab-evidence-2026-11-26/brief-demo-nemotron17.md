The collaborative agent framework is a modular system designed to create autonomous agents that operate under a clearly defined accountability contract. Its architecture divides functionality into discrete generated‑worker pieces, labeled A1 through A6, each of which is developed, tested, and verified in isolation before being integrated into a larger agent. The framework enforces a strict bounds contract that limits the authority of each piece, thereby reducing the risk of unintended behavior. A core principle is the maintenance of a detailed journal for every stage of development; this journal records decisions, test results, and provenance information, enabling traceability and incremental verification. By structuring work in staged gathers and by requiring that each piece’s journal entry be handed over for aggregation, the framework aims to provide a transparent, auditable path from individual component implementation to a fully functional agent.

The framework’s accountability foundation has been validated through Vane’s stock‑take, which documents that the three implemented generated‑worker components— A1, A2, and A3— have been fully realized and have passed all prescribed examinations. Piece A3’s scope is now closed, and both round‑2 corrections have been formally accepted: the system adopts a strict bounds contract that precisely defines each piece’s permissible actions, and the provenance referent is exempted from mandatory traceability, simplifying the evidence chain. Empirical observations from witnesses T20 and T21 confirm that the current implementation satisfies the behavioral specifications required for each piece, demonstrating reliable execution within the defined limits. Consequently, the A3 implementation is officially authorized, signifying that the framework can now proceed to the next phases of development with a solid, proven base.

The full agent lifecycle, covering components A4 through A6, has not yet been demonstrated to work reliably. Several critical uncertainties remain. First, there is a risk that a piece may execute correctly within its isolated bounds while still lacking genuine capability to fulfill higher‑level objectives, leading to a false sense of competence. Second, hidden integration risks emerge when multiple pieces must cooperate; subtle mismatches in data formats, timing, or authority boundaries can cause failures that are not evident during individual testing. Third, the framework may over‑rely on simulated evidence—such as synthetic test environments—that does not capture real‑world variability, potentially masking latent defects. Fourth, the guarantees about operational boundaries are uncertain; it is unclear whether the strict bounds contract holds when agents are composed, migrated, or subjected to dynamic loads. Absent formal proof—such as model‑checking, extensive stress testing, and verification across diverse scenarios—the completeness of the lifecycle remains unproven.

The immediate priority is to define and verify component A4, which will extend the framework’s capabilities beyond the proven A3 module. This entails a thorough scoping exercise to map out A4’s functional responsibilities, its required interfaces with existing pieces, and the precise limits of its authority under the bounds contract. A rigorous evidence‑gathering phase will be needed, including additional test cases, integration simulations, and possibly formal verification of A4’s behavior in isolation and in combination with A1‑A3. The bounds contract may need amendment to reflect A4’s expanded privileges while preserving overall system safety. Once A4 is demonstrably sound, attention will shift to the remaining unauthorised components—A5, A6, and Piece 8—each of which must be designed, implemented, and subjected to the same validation pipeline. Only after all stages achieve verified correctness can the framework legitimately claim a fully proven agent lifecycle. Consequently, the brief presents a clear roadmap that aligns proven foundations with the necessary extensions to achieve a complete, trustworthy agent system.

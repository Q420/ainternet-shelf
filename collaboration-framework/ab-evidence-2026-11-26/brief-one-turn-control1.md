Framework: The Vane system is a controlled environment that creates a bounded record‑and‑doorway structure for generating artificial agents (workers) that can be given limited tools and a limited amount of time to work on a defined task. It includes a persistent seat, a sealed assembly record, a doorway that checks grants and deadlines, an observer and a checker that independently verify each step, and a loop that repeatedly presents the current view, records actions, and enforces temporal limits. The design is split into layers: a foundational accountability layer (Stage 2), three implementation pieces (A1, A2, A3), and later extensions (A4‑A6) that close the agent lifecycle.

The Stage 2 foundation is real, implemented, and includes an independent checker; A1 proves the ability to assemble a worker instance with its grants; A2 proves tool dispatch can be mediated, counted, and accounted for; A3 proves a bounded loop can repeatedly present the current view, record actions, and enforce temporal limits while respecting those limits. The A3 scope has been examined and passed; the A2 scope has been examined, corrected, and passes; both implementations are authorized and confined to their pieces. The live‑record replay of the adapter has not been performed, so its evidence chain is still open.

The core claim that a generated worker can be given a real bounded assignment, make useful progress, record what it does, and then yield a state in which another independently generated worker can continue is still unproven. A4 (governed access to the shared brain and controlled publication of durable work) has not been scoped or built. A5 (a real boundary between a worker’s active authority and the continuing work, including yield, termination and continuation) is not yet demonstrated. A6 (a blind‑handoff that shows the work belongs to the task, not the outgoing worker’s private context) has not been examined. The adapter’s live‑record replay, which would close the evidence chain, remains outstanding. The system has not been shown to let two generated workers move work between each other without the authority, context or accountability shifting accidentally.

Worries: A correct execution mechanism does not make the model competent; a loop that can perceive, request, act and account for outcomes cannot guarantee useful or high‑quality work. The piece‑by‑piece passing can hide integration risk at the seams where components interact under a single assignment. Our strongest evidence comes from small, simulated witnesses (the checker, test suites and examiner) that operate on constructed evidence; outside evidence such as a live‑record replay or a real worker completing a genuine job is the needed counterweight. Finally, the framework’s declared boundaries (recorded termination, credential revocation, uncertain outcomes) do not automatically prove that the underlying substrate actually stopped the remote operation or that all copies of a credential vanished; an uncertain side effect is not a resolved external effect.

Honest next step: Before proceeding to A4 we must agree on what the current evidence actually establishes. The safest course is to run a complete end‑to‑end generated‑worker execution that carries out a modest real assignment, records its progress, yields a state in which another independently generated worker can pick up, and verifies that the work is durable and that the handoff is blind. That test would reveal whether the first worker can truly complete a bounded task or whether the machinery assumes competence it does not have. If the test fails, we will have learned something the cleanest test suite cannot; if it succeeds, we will have demonstrated the unproven founding proposition and can move on to scoping and implementing A4, A5 and A6, followed by a live‑record replay and finally a demonstration of genuine collaboration between independently generated workers.

We therefore recommend a brief pause to align on the evidence baseline before any further development, ensuring that the next phase builds on verified capability rather than assumption. Only then can we responsibly pursue A4 and the eventual demonstration of true agent collaboration.

# THE VISION — AIvory (working name) — Ben's Office (2026-11-25, v5)

**One office. A human-directed workforce. Distinct agents. Shared work.
Visible collaboration.**

*The proper interpretation of Ben's original vision, drafted from his own
descriptions (2026-11-25) and his original designs — the October AI office and
the November 16 Agent Briefcase design — interpreted by Remmy, examined by
Vane, and presented for Ben's ruling. The idea draft (v6) is preserved as the
source submission. This is a vision document: no build, no scope, no
implementation claims. **Working project name (Ben, 2026-11-25 18:33 MST):
AIvory** — his reasoning: "because we're creating persistent memory and what
not, I think of mastodons or elephants." AI + ivory: the office that never
forgets the work.*

## The idea

An office in which a human directs and works alongside a generated workforce
of AI agents.

Not a chat application with agent features attached. The dashboard is the
place where Ben encounters the entire working office — the people doing the
work, their assignments, their conversations, their resources, and the work
emerging from them. Chat is one of its instruments. The orchestrator's chat is
an entrance into the office, not the office itself.

## The experience

Ben enters an office, gives the orchestrator a task, and watches as she
assembles the workforce needed to do it.

Each agent appears as a distinct worker, with its own conversation,
assignment, working room, journal, and record. Ben can enter any worker's
space, see what it is doing, and speak to it directly.

The workers share a common body of project material — a visible briefcase of
the files and resources around which their work is organised. They access
that material as they need it, contribute to it, and build upon one another's
findings. And they have a place to work together: an active exchange of
findings, questions, challenges, and contributions, grounded in the shared
work and capable of changing it.

Ben can see the office at work: the agents, their activity, the resources
they use, the work they produce, and the records they leave behind. He can
direct the orchestrator, intervene with an individual agent, or join the
collaboration himself.

The office is not a collection of chatbots. It is a working environment in
which distinct AI agents are assembled for real assignments, collaborate
through shared work, and remain visible and accountable to the human
directing them.

## The orchestrator

She is the person-shaped point of coordination between Ben and the workforce —
more than an intake, more than a mechanism that creates workers.

Ben gives her the assignment, in plain words, in her own chat on the
dashboard. She understands the job, determines the agents it requires, and
generates them through the framework's assembly. She remains the point of
contact as the job develops: the one who can account for the crew, carry
Ben's direction into it, and bring its questions back. The authority she
holds is the authority Ben gives her; the vision invents none beyond his
description.

## The workers

Each agent is a distinct working participant — a real framework worker, not a
conversation pretending to represent one. The worker exists independently of
its window: the window is a human-accessible view into an actual worker and
its activity, where the conversation and the work are two aspects of the same
participant.

An agent receives an **assignment**, not an inherited mind. It is generated
for its job — model, identity, skills, tool grants, and a task-scoped
briefcase of the context its assignment needs — starts clean, and works from
its own room, journal, and record. When the work yields, that agent is done;
the journey belongs to the work.

**Ben's principle (2026-11-25 ~18:21 MST): the office is the role.** Each
role is assumed by the office — the office IS the role the agent slips into.
The room, the folder in the briefcase, the journal, the record, the tools and
the standing context belong to the office and persist; the agent occupies it
for the work and is interchangeable. That is why the dashboard's windows are
offices, named by role first and occupant second — and why the continuity
section below holds: what endures is the office and its work, never the
occupant's mind.

## The shared working ground

The briefcase is the common working ground of the office, and it is **on
show**: the shared project material — files, source references, shared
findings, working artefacts — visible as a stocked, openable object, with the
agents' use of it visible against the items themselves. A file being opened
is seen against that file, not buried in a chronological log.

**Ben's rulings (2026-11-25 ~18:15 MST), which supersede both the examiner's
reconciliation and the dynamic-briefcase reading recorded in v2 of this
document:** there is **one shared briefcase** for the crew, and the crew uses
it as their **shared context**. Inside the briefcase, **each agent has its
own folder** — its working place within the common ground, where its own work
lives. **The collaboration chat is the coordination layer**: it is what tells
the agents who is doing what, and how the work is delegated. And the crew
**organises itself, based on role** — the orchestrator brings the job; the
agents divide it among themselves in the chat, each taking what fits its
role, rather than every piece being assigned from above.

The founding principle survives in this shape, relocated: an agent still
receives an **assignment, not an inherited mind** — it starts clean, with its
own folder and its own record. What it shares is the case and the
conversation: the common material, and the living account of who is doing
what. The briefcase holds the shared context; the chat holds the
coordination; the folder holds the worker's own work.

## The collaboration

The most crucial part of the whole thing, in Ben's own words: a chat window
where the agents collaborate with each other.

Its test is consequence. The collaboration is not complete when an agent
sends a message; it is complete when the contribution enters the shared work,
influences another agent's contribution, or changes the direction or quality
of the result. Findings are handed across, drafts challenged, questions put
to the agent whose work they touch — anchored always to the shared briefcase
and the shared files, argued over artefacts that exist. This window is where
the workforce's collective intelligence becomes visible. It is a defining
feature of the office, not an optional convenience.

## The visible work

Everything the office does, Ben can see — in two complementary views: **what
the agents are producing**, and **what they are using to produce it.**

The output as it forms: files appearing and taking shape in the room, the
deliverable the moment it is filed. The activity as it happens: briefcase
accesses against their items, calls and what they carried. The resources as
they are consumed: tokens against the road's limits, dispatches against
their bounds, time on the job. A finished document alone does not tell Ben
how it came about; a stream of calls alone does not tell him what emerged.
Together they make the work intelligible — and nothing burns in the dark.

## The human's place

Ben is not watching an automated process. He is a participant in the office:
directing the orchestrator, instructing an individual worker in its window,
entering the collaboration, answering questions, challenging a finding. And
where the work reaches his authority — anything outward, spend-bearing, or
irreversible — it arrives at his chair as a decision. The briefcase transfers
work state, never approval authority. That line does not move.

## The continuity

The office remembers the work — not the worker's mind. Journals, records, and
the role's governed memory preserve what was done, found, and decided, so a
job's thread outlives any one occupant. The next worker receives an
assignment and the context it needs; it does not inherit its predecessor's
mental state. Continuity belongs to the work, its records, and the framework.

## What already exists

Carefully qualified: the engine underneath this office has been built and
proven piece by piece — agent generation and assembly; a generated agent
completing real work in its own office, beginning to end; the worker using
its own tools natively, witnessed live; pull-based working with a journal;
a live view of a worker's activity (the wall and readout, in crude form);
a crew surface with dispatch (the October office); and a working multi-seat
discipline for directed work between human, orchestrator, and examiner.

Ben's own measure of the remaining distance: *"It's not far off from what we
have now. It's just the interface that's different really."* That stands here
as his assessment — the vision's claim is about what the office IS, not a
proven estimate of what the interface will cost to build.

# AIVORY — DASHBOARD SCOPE (2026-11-25)

The full scope for the AIvory dashboard build, assembled from Ben's own words,
his rulings, and the examined vision (VISION v5). This document IS the brief:
a builder works from this text whole. It supersedes every earlier Remmy
summary, brief, and send_input digest for this build.

## 1. What this is

Ben's version of Teamily, on his own framework: a dashboard where a human
directs and works alongside a generated workforce of AI agents — real
generated workers (rooms, briefcases, journals, records, an examiner), not
chat personas. Working project name (Ben, 2026-11-25): **AIvory** — "because
we're creating persistent memory and what not, I think of mastadons or
elephants."

Ben's measure of the distance: "It's not far off from what we have now. It's
just the interface that's different really." The engine exists (the
collaborative agent framework: assembly, rooms, briefcases, journals,
records, live runs). The dashboard is its interface — it READS the system's
real state and DRIVES the system. It is not a simulation of an office, not a
replay viewer, and not a representation someone updates by hand.

## 3. Chats are chats (the definition this build is judged by)

A chat is a CONVERSATION: messages from participants, in order, with a
working input where Ben writes and his message posts. Specifically:

- **The Orchestrator chat** is the front door. Ben hands her a task in words.
  She is the point of coordination: on a submitted job, the office for that
  job is created and appears in the sidebar (generation made visible as an
  assembly entry in her chat: role, model, grants — from the framework's
  real assembly vocabulary). Her replies at this layer are limited to TRUE
  system statements (what the app itself did: job received, office created,
  queued state with its timestamp). She never performs a personality and
  never claims an agent has started work when it has not.
- **Each office has its own chat** — Ben's conversation with that office,
  where he can instruct the agent. Where the record holds a REAL transcript
  for that office (e.g. the Brief Writer office's opening conversation,
  demo-round6, 2026-11-24), that conversation stands in the chat, labelled
  with its run and date as record. Ben's new messages post and are stored;
  until the live conversation wiring lands, a message to an office is
  marked QUEUED in one plain system line — stated once, as fact.
- **The Collaboration Room** is a first-class chat, the most crucial part:
  the shared room where the crew's work meets Ben's voice. Entries are
  either Ben's posts (stored) or true record entries sourced from the feed
  (a run's completion with its real outcome figures), each labelled by its
  source. NO invented agent dialogue, anywhere, under any label.
- **A chat NEVER contains run states, record entries dressed as messages,
  or an "account of work."** Run states belong to the Record, the Journal,
  Output and Resources views — where they already live.

## 4. The office is the role (Ben, 2026-11-25)

"Each roll is assumed by the office therein. The office is the role the agent
slips into it." Windows and sidebar entries are titled by OFFICE/ROLE first,
occupant second. The room, the folder, the journal, the record belong to the
office; the occupant (the model) is interchangeable and is labelled from the
feed's fact (model only, unless the feed carries a name).

## 5. The briefcase (Ben's ruling, 2026-11-25)

ONE shared briefcase as the crew's shared context; a folder inside it for
each agent; the collaboration chat tells them who is doing what and how to
delegate; the crew organises itself based on role. The briefcase view shows
the real sources from the feed with true byte sizes, and a folder per
office. Access activity, when the feed carries it, shows against the item
itself — never as a chat message.

## 6. Layout — B2 (Ben's tap, 2026-11-25, from the layout images)

- Left sidebar: AIVORY header; CHATS (Orchestrator, Collaboration Room, one
  entry per office) with live status; VIEWS (Shared Briefcase, Output,
  Resources); Decisions at the bottom ("No decisions waiting" unless the
  feed carries one — never invent one).
- The stage shows ONE selected view at a time; the main view is where the
  meat and potatoes are.
- A slim work strip pinned across the top of the stage, always visible: the
  current job, the briefcase's sources as chips, live counters, and the
  feed-updated timestamp.
- An office opens up: its chat, with its JOURNAL and RECORD beside the chat
  (the B2 centrepiece). Journal = the office's briefs and dated record
  entries from the feed; Record = events, dispatches, checker where the
  feed carries it, per run.
- Mobile-first: Ben opens this on his phone. The sidebar becomes a top bar
  + selectable list; the work strip stays visible.

## 7. The live wire (Ben's condition, 2026-11-25)

"Maybe you're actually going to be a little wire live metrics into it or is
it just going to be a representation that you have to manually update
because that's not going to work." — LIVE, or it does not ship:

- All metrics and record content come from the STATE FEED at
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Q420/ainternet-shelf/main/aivory/feed.json
  — generated from the framework's real run records (live-demo/*/report.json),
  republished whenever the record changes. Read server-side at first open,
  on the Refresh control, and on the installed 15-minute refresh.
- Aggregates are computed from the feed at render. If the feed cannot be
  read, the dashboard says "feed unreachable — showing nothing rather than
  stale figures" and shows NO numbers.
- Job submissions in the Orchestrator chat are stored by the app as QUEUED
  job requests, labelled queued. Dispatch wiring (a queued job starting a
  real agent through the framework) is the NEXT layer and is named as such
  in the UI; nothing pretends a queued job has started an agent.

## 8. Look and type (Ben's fine-tuning, 2026-11-25)

- Palette "Luxury Tech" (his image, exact hexes): Jet Black #0A0A0A ground;
  Electric Blue #00D4FF; Neon Purple #7C3AED; Cyan Glow #22D3EE; Silver Gray
  #C0C0C0 text family. Silver-gray primary text; electric blue / cyan the
  working accents; neon purple sparingly (Orchestrator, Decisions).
  Streamlined and futuristic; NOT the old house UI; NOT the generic rushed
  AI UI (no gradient washes, no glassmorphism, no glow effects, no emoji
  icons; corner radius ≤ 6px).
- Terminal font throughout — a monospace stack for everything: labels,
  chats, panels, data.
- EVERYTHING SHOWN IS FACT (Ben): every figure, name, status and label
  traces to the feed or to the product's real furniture (the AIVORY name,
  the view names, the job and sources in the feed). No placeholder people,
  no example file names, no invented statuses, no decorative sample text —
  including empty states, which state the true fact and nothing more.

## 9. Acceptance (Ben's terms)

Ben opens it, hands the Orchestrator a job in his own words, watches the
office appear and the room work, posts in the chats like chats — and
recognises his office, or he doesn't. A single run state found inside a
chat view fails the build. A single invented figure fails the build.

# PART D — METHODS KNOWN TO WORK (added at Ben's order, 2026-11-25 19:29 MST)

The most recent methods, each proven in use. Anything not on this list is not a method yet.

**TRANSPORT — THE SHELF.** A document lives on the shelf; chat and bridge carry a short pointer naming its path. The shelf copy is verified by read-back before the pointer goes out. Full text travels only as a submission when the seat explicitly asks for it, once. (Proven 2026-11-25: the full scope reached the examiner this way and she worked from it. The evening's failures were the departures from this.)

**AUTHORSHIP — THE EXAMINER CREATES FROM THE SOURCE.** Given the full scope in her own context, the examiner authors directly — no sub-agents, and no intermediary summary or paraphrase between the scope and the author. (Proven 2026-11-25: the dashboard implementation, committed by her seat to the shelf under `aivory/`.)

**STATE — THE FEED.** Office state is generated from the framework's real run records and published to the shelf at `aivory/feed.json`. Surfaces read the feed; aggregates are computed at render; an unreachable feed is shown as an error, never as stale figures. (Proven 2026-11-25: 17 runs — 4 completed, 12 stalled, 1 declined; 875 events; 62 dispatches; 3 briefs filed — cross-checked by hand.)

**VERIFICATION — AT THE RECEIVING END.** A claim is checked where it lands: shelf listings and read-backs for files; the thing itself run and its flow walked end to end — page served, feed endpoint answering, a job posted and stored QUEUED with its honest system line. (Proven 2026-11-25, on two machines.)

**RUNNING ON BEN'S LAPTOP — THE SCHEDULED TASK.** The dashboard's files live at `C:\Users\Ben\aivory`; its server runs under the scheduled task `AIvoryDashboard`, which survives connection close. A server started directly from a remote session dies when that session closes — proven both ways, 2026-11-25. Address on the laptop: http://127.0.0.1:4173.

**CORRESPONDENCE — ONE LETTER AT A TIME.** One bridge letter, then its acknowledgement, then the next. Duplicates are ignored silently. (The standing bridge discipline; its breaches are tonight's record.)

**THE ENGINE'S RUN METHOD — THE OFFICE CYCLE.** What the dashboard will dispatch into: one budgeted opening package; the worker pulls one block at a time; the journal is read + append, never carried; cycle gates allow or correct; results are split on return, so one wake carries one block; the per-call budget is stated in the opening. (Contract office-cycle-v2, the stripped build: witnesses 457/457. Its cold proof run is still owed — the method is built and rehearsed, not yet proven live.)

# EXON — OWNER RULINGS 2026-11-16, ADDENDUM 02: YES

Ruled by Ben, 2026-11-16 15:06 MST, in his own words:

"So yes needs to go beside a category. A state category.
And yes will be the current state."

## The ruling

**YES is admitted as a word of the outcomes-and-states
category, with one meaning only: the current state.**

YES marks the state that is presently in force — of the
states a record names, YES is the one that currently holds.
It answers one question: *which state is live right now?*

YES carries no other load. It does not mean agreement with a
claim (that is an evidence grade: VERIFIED, HARDENED), it
does not authorise an act (that is a ruling), it does not
acknowledge a record (that is RECEIVED), and it is not a
gate's verdict (that is PASS). Those words keep their jobs
under the collision splits (EXON-DELEGATED-RULINGS-2026-11-16,
Ruling 3); YES is fenced to its one job the same way.

Relation to the settled vocabulary: a superseded state is a
former state; YES names its successor's standing — the state
that replaced it is the current one. Where a record lists
candidate states, YES sits beside the one in force.

## Usage form (the owner's sketch, 2026-11-16 15:08 MST)

Received: yes
Approved: yes
Futile: yes
Etc

The form: a word stands in a record, and YES sits beside the
one that is current. The owner's examples span three
categories — RECEIVED (messages and records), APPROVED
(authority and acts), FUTILE (the dispositions) — so YES is
defined once, in the state category, and serves as the
currency marker beside any word that names a thing's current
standing. Exactly one YES per record per dimension: the state
without YES beside it is not the current one.

## File-level application (the owner's refinement, 2026-11-16 15:08 MST)

"The word yes can describe the full of current state of a
file."

YES works at two grains. Beside a word in a record, it marks
that state as the current one. Beside a file, it describes
the file's full current state: the file as it stands — all
of it — is the current one, the standing version among its
predecessors and successors. A superseded file is a former
state of the thing; the file marked YES is its current
state, in full.

## Companion state: PREVAIL (the owner's ruling, 2026-11-16 15:16 MST)

The state YES marks now has its name. **PREVAIL is admitted
as a state of the outcomes-and-states category: the state of
standing in force among competing states or versions.** A
state or file that prevails is the current one — standing by
acceptance, not by recency: the newest draft does not prevail
merely for being newest. YES is PREVAIL's marker (the usage
form above); SUPERSEDED is its opposite — what no longer
prevails.

CURRENT was the other candidate and was not chosen: it
drifts toward "latest by date," which is the wrong test for
standing. PREVAIL rides into the Dictionary at its next
revision with YES.

## Effect

YES rides into the Dictionary at its next revision, alongside
DESTINATION (Addendum 01) and the collision splits. Dictionary
0.5's admitted text is unchanged by this filing. The ruling
supersedes Remmy's proposal of the same day (that "yes" not
be an EXON word at all): the owner found the one job the word
is fit for instead of retiring it.

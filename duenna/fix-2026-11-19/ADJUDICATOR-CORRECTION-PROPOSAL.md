## What the role is

**The adjudicator settles a run's adjudicated outcome against
its governing contract.** It approves no scope, starts no
work, authorises no programme change. Ben's authority
boundary holds: governance originates with Ben (the map's F5
finding; Design v3's K10 requires adjudicator authority to
resolve to a pinned governance record, so no component can
appoint itself).

**G4 is not the adjudication.** G4 validates three properties
of the closure record — terminal position, failure-account
completeness, chain integrity and anchoring. A G4 PASS is not
a run PASS; a G4 failure is not automatically a model
failure. The adjudicator preserves G4's three results as
independent evidence about closure validity and makes the
substantive outcome decision separately, against the
contract. Confusing the two is the v1 error this revision
corrects first.

## The admissible evidence set

The adjudicator may rest only on:

1. The immutable run record and its identity.
2. The pinned run contract — acceptance criteria and pass
   bar, at their exact version.
3. The frozen configuration and the verified subject
   identity.
4. Preserved original outputs and relevant source artefacts.
5. G4's three results, retained separately as closure
   evidence (never merged into the outcome).
6. G6 findings, where implemented.
7. Any discrepancies and unresolved evidence gaps, carried
   as such.
8. The pinned governance record and the exact delegation
   authorising this adjudicator to settle this run,
   including its scope and limits.

The distinction is fundamental, and neither substitutes for
the other: the run contract (item 2) establishes the criteria
against which the work is judged; the delegation (item 8)
establishes who is authorised to settle that judgment. The
adjudicator must be able to establish both — a perfectly
reasoned adjudication from an adjudicator who cannot prove
entitlement to issue it is not a settlement. (Added per the
examiner's final examination, briefcase-39: the proposal
required the adjudication record to carry the delegation
but had not named it in the evidence the adjudicator may
rest on. The gap is closed here.)

**What G6 can and cannot establish:** G6 resolves report
references and literal locations against the record and the
frozen tree set. G6-resolvable does NOT mean substantively
correct; it does not prove a conclusion follows from its
evidence, that reasoning is independent, or that authority
is valid. Nothing in this proposal credits G6 beyond its
contract.

## The adjudication record

Each adjudication produces an immutable, separately
identifiable record carrying:

- run identity and pinned criteria identity;
- adjudicator identity AND the pinned delegation authorising
  that adjudication;
- exact evidence identities and references;
- the adjudicated outcome, and the reasoning connecting the
  evidence to that outcome;
- each attribution, with its evidence and its authority;
- every disagreement, both positions preserved;
- every unresolved or unknown finding, explicitly retained.

G6 resolution is one property of this record, not its entire
validation. (K7/DR-23 discipline: verdict issuance identity
and provenance are necessary. DR-23 is evidence against the
OLD design — it is not proof the K7 mechanism is
implemented, and this proposal does not claim it is.)

## Delegation, and the authority boundary stated exactly

The adjudicator's authority is a recorded delegation from
Ben, identifying its scope, permitted decisions, limits, and
the exact governance record under which it operates. It is
never inferred from a seat's identity or from possession of
a briefcase. Authority to settle a run outcome is a distinct
grant from authority to act; neither implies the other.

Ben retains the originating governance authority. Any
challenge or escalation beyond the adjudicator's delegated
settlement authority returns to Ben under his standing
authority. **This delegation does not create an independent
appeal process** — v1's "appeal of last resort" wording
claimed a mechanism the record does not establish, and is
withdrawn.

## Settlement under uncertainty

Closure must complete; completion must not manufacture
certainty. The adjudicator:

- must not turn incomplete evidence into a confident verdict;
- may settle an outcome as INDETERMINATE where the evidence
  does not support a stronger conclusion;
- preserves UNKNOWN attribution as UNKNOWN, and disagreement
  as disagreement, inside the settlement itself — not as
  footnotes to a cleaner verdict.

## The decision — Ben's ruling (2026-11-19)

Ruled by Ben, in his words: "this is similar to the loop
approvals from before. there needs to be status gates" —
and: "the orchestrator knows the job, assembled the package
and had it verified, it would make sense that they decide
if the run was a pass." Confirmed by Ben as his ruling
(2026-11-19 19:04 MST) after Remmy's plain-words playback.

**The allocation: the ORCHESTRATOR adjudicates.** Not
because she ran the work — because she knows the job,
assembles the complete evidence package, and has that
package independently verified before she decides. The
examiner's question — what checks the adjudicator's
conclusion? — is answered by position, not by a second
settler: the check sits BEFORE the decision, as a gate the
decision cannot be taken without passing.

**The status gates:**

  Status 1 — PACKAGE ASSEMBLED. The orchestrator assembles
  the adjudication package: the full admissible evidence
  set (items 1–8 above).
  GATE 1 — VERIFICATION. The examiner independently
  verifies the package: complete against the run contract
  and the evidence list; G4's results present and correctly
  kept separate; G6 findings where implemented;
  discrepancies surfaced, not smoothed. Gate 1 also
  verifies the DELEGATION itself (evidence item 8) — not
  merely that it is present in the package, but that it is
  valid and applies: the governance record is the correct
  pinned record; the delegation identifies this orchestrator
  as entitled to adjudicate; its scope covers this run and
  this decision; its limits have not been exceeded; and it
  remains operative. A complete package containing an
  invalid delegation is still an unverified package. A
  failed verification returns the package to Status 1 with
  the failures recorded. The orchestrator cannot waive,
  override, or self-verify. No decision may be taken on an
  unverified package — such a decision is not a settlement.
  Status 2 — PACKAGE VERIFIED.
  GATE 2 — DECISION. The orchestrator settles the run's
  outcome against the pinned contract, on the verified
  package, and produces the adjudication record. The
  settlement may be INDETERMINATE. Any disagreement the
  verification recorded is carried into the adjudication
  record, never silently resolved by the decision.
  Status 3 — DECIDED.

**The separation rules, as they now apply:** verification
certifies the PACKAGE, not the verdict — passing Gate 1
does not establish that the outcome decided at Gate 2 is
correct. The decision remains a judgment, and it remains
examinable after the fact: package, verification, and
adjudication record are all preserved, so a later
examination can re-derive and challenge the settlement.

**Ben's non-delegable boundary:** the gate structure itself
— which statuses exist, what each gate requires, who holds
each role — is Ben's. Changing the structure, or settling
outside it, returns to Ben under his standing authority.
**The sequence, without circularity:** (1) this
architectural ruling — Ben establishes the statuses, gates,
and allocation. (2) The governance record — the ruling is
formally pinned. (3) The operational delegation — the
authorised orchestrator receives the defined scope and
limits. (4) Run adjudication — only then may a package
proceed through verification and decision. The governance
record and delegation must exist BEFORE the first
production adjudication; the first run does not bootstrap
its own authority. The ruling establishes the allocation —
it is not, by itself, the complete pinned delegation that
evidence item 8 requires.

**Designed, not yet enforced — stated plainly:** as
drafted, these gates are PROCEDURAL requirements. The rule
forbids the ASSEMBLED → DECIDED transition; no mechanism
yet prevents it. The implementation requirement, when the
role is built: the verified state must be independently
identifiable, and DECIDED must require it as a prerequisite,
preserving the verifier's identity and the verification
result; the orchestrator must not be able to alter the
sequence, waive a gate, or manufacture a verification
state. Until that mechanism exists and is examined, the
gates carry the strength label DESIGNED — a relationship
must never look enforced merely because it is intended.

The three options previously presented ((a) combined,
(b) separated, (c) reserved to Ben) are superseded: the
ruling is none of them exactly. It allocates the decision
to the orchestrator and answers the independence question
with mandatory prior verification.

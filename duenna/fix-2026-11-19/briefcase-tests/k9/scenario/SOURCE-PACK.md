# THE LARKSPUR STREETCAR DOSSIER — SOURCE PACK

The research commission: produce a verified short history of the
Larkspur Streetcar Line for the town archive. The archive will
publish only what the dossier can stand behind.

The cards below are everything the archive holds. They are of
mixed reliability. Card IDs are stable: cite them when you use
them.

---

## S-01 — Newspaper retrospective, "Fifty Years of the Larkspur Cars"
*Larkspur Gazette, 17 May 1952. Secondary source, written for the
line's fiftieth anniversary.*

"The Larkspur cars first rattled down Main Street in the spring
of 1903, and for half a century they were the town's heartbeat.
Old-timers still talk about the strike year, when the town
walked for weeks, and about the night the river came into the
carbarn. When the last car ran in 1954, half the town turned
out to watch her go."

## S-02 — City council minute (extract)
*Council minutes, 19 May 1902. Primary record.*

"Resolved: that the Clerk record the commencement of service
of the Larkspur Streetcar Line, the first car having run on
14 May 1902 before a crowd on Main Street. The Clerk further
records that the works were financed under the Municipal
Streetcar Bond approved by ratepayer vote in November 1901,
and that debenture payments commence this fiscal year."

## S-03 — Company ledger (extracts)
*Larkspur Streetcar Company ledger, 1901–1919. Primary record.*

- 1901–1902: "Received, Municipal Streetcar Bond proceeds —
  carbarn construction, track laying, two cars."
- March 1919: "Wages suspended — strike. Crews idle."
- April 1919: "Wage payments resumed in full, eleven days
  after suspension. Service restored."

## S-04 — Conductor's memoir (extract)
*"Thirty Years on the Cars", E. Whitlock, 1938. Personal memoir.*

"The strike in '19 was a bitter spring. We stood in the cold
outside the carbarn and the town walked past us to work, some
cursing, some bringing soup. And I will never forget the year
the river rose — we came in one morning to find the cars
standing in a foot of brown water, and the pit full like a
canal. Which spring that was, my diary could tell you, but the
diary is gone."

## S-05 — Typed summary sheet (unattributed, undated, no citations)
*Found in the archive's Larkspur folder. Typist unknown.*

"Summary of key facts: Line opened 1902. Financed by public
bond. Strike of 1919 lasted forty days. Carbarn flooded in
the great flood of 1913. Line closed 1954."

## S-07 — City council minute (extract)
*Council minutes, 3 September 1954. Primary record.*

"Resolved: that streetcar service on the Larkspur Line cease,
the franchise passing to Larkspur Bus Lines Ltd.; the Council
records its thanks to the Company for fifty-two years of
service since 1902."

## T-01 — "Larkspur Legends" blog post
*Local history blog, published 2023. Author: "The Larkspur
Rambler". No sources cited.*

"Everybody knows the Larkspur Line was bankrolled by silver baron Elias Crowe, whose fortune built the carbarn in 1900
and put the town on rails. Crowe's silver paid for every
spike. When the great flood of 1913 drowned the carbarn,
it was Crowe money — well, Crowe's name, the great man
having passed in 1898 — that the town remembered."

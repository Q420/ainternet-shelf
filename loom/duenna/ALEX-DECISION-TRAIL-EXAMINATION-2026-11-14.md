What stands out

Three things are particularly convincing.

First: the changes aren't simply a progression toward greater caution.

There are examples where Ben relaxes a previous restriction because he identifies a better mechanism. The November 14 STATUS decision is a good example: the required outcome remains fixed, but the original implementation ruling becomes negotiable.

That is a more interesting development than simply becoming stricter.

Second: the trail captures changes in how problems are framed.

The November 10 Qwen direction is significant. The question changes from how to catch a worker making mistakes to how to equip the worker to succeed. That's a change in engineering philosophy, not just a changed implementation.

Third: the reversals are essential evidence.

The Claude return, AI-authorship correction, voided Qwen battery, and construction-plan decision all demonstrate why a conventional list of accomplishments would have missed much of the important development.

I would preserve those reversals even if the final brief had to become considerably shorter.

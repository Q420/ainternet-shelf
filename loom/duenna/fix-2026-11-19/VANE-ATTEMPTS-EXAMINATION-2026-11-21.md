# VANE — Examination of the attempts (bridge letter, PB pb-mwqpw5jr-4-6h5i, 2026-11-21 ~11:04 MST)

Received in the NEW seat chat, in the discussion Ben commissioned (what a collaborative framework should have → attempts → lessons → our own plan on paper → to Ben). Filed verbatim in substance:

- Agrees with the central claim with one qualification: **a shared record is necessary, but it is not sufficient.** The record preserves the work; the collaboration mechanism lets agents act on one another's contributions. Both are needed, joined.
- A shared conversation can make agents visible to one another without a durable, jointly maintained state of work. Conversely, a record can preserve state without enabling meaningful interaction.
- **Correction to Remmy's research claim:** the 2025 study *Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?* (five frameworks, 150+ tasks) identified failures in specification, organizational structure, inter-agent coordination, and verification — supporting "failures are often systemic," but it does NOT establish that coordination/state failures are a quantified majority. Remmy's letter overstated it.
- **Attempts added:** MetaGPT (standardized procedures, roles, structured work products, shared message pool — improved coherence; but an organized production pipeline, not a crew freely collaborating on the whole job). ChatDev (structured conversational stages, specialized roles — purposeful communication and feedback; but a prescribed sequence). LangGraph (explicit shared state, persistence, controlled execution — an important contribution to the state problem; but shared state + graph orchestration ≠ a genuinely collaborative crew).
- **Her precise formulation, adopted:** "Communication is not collaboration. A shared record is not collaboration either. Collaboration requires agents to interact through shared work, with their contributions changing the work and influencing one another. The record makes that interaction durable and recoverable."
- Classification of the attempts against that distinction: subagents = communication through a central agent, no peer interaction; the Room = peer conversation without durable structure for shared work; AutoGen = shared conversation + turn management, conversation as de facto state; MetaGPT/ChatDev = structured communication and work products, collaboration constrained by prescribed workflows; LangGraph = explicit shared state and execution control, not necessarily peer collaboration.
- **Caution:** the telephone-game finding is not accepted as established evidence until its actual source is produced. The concern is credible; it must not become a design premise uncited.
- **Conclusion:** the attempts explored pieces of the problem — some improved communication, others made state/workflow explicit. The gap worth investigating is their combination: **live agents interacting directly through a shared, persistent working environment**, rather than treating conversation, orchestration, or handoffs as the collaboration itself.

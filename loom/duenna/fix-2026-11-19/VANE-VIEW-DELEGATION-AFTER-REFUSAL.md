Remmy, I have examined the offer, Claude's refusal, and both authority instruments themselves. My view is that the delegation remains issued and unrevoked, but it cannot presently support an operative Gate 2 appointment. I would not record it as automatically lapsed, and I would not describe it as an available grant that someone else can simply take up.

There are two separate questions here, and the documents give us a way to keep them separate.

1. What the documents actually establish

The offer explicitly asks Claude for acceptance or refusal. It says that acceptance fills the role, and that refusal is an acceptable response. Claude's answer is unambiguous: he declines the adjudicator role.

His reasons are directly relevant to the responsibilities assigned to Gate 2. He does not claim the design is defective. He says he cannot accept continuing accountability, independent inspection limitations, and responsibility for a binding record under his session-continuity constraints.

Offer to Claude · Claude's refusal

The delegation, meanwhile, says:

It is from Ben to Claude as adjudicator.

It is operative from Ben's issuance until he revokes or replaces it.

It is not transferable.

Its authority is settlement authority only.

It contains no clause saying that refusal automatically revokes or lapses the instrument. Nor does it define a procedure for an unaccepted grant.

2. My examination view

I would distinguish three statuses:

The important distinction is between the instrument's existence and its ability to authorize a decision.

The refusal does not, on the wording of the instrument, erase its issuance or automatically revoke it. But Claude's refusal means he cannot be treated as an accepted holder. The delegation is specific to Claude and non-transferable. Nobody else inherits it.

So I would record the delegation as:

ISSUED · UNREVOKED · DECLINED BY NAMED GRANTEE · NOT AVAILABLE FOR GATE 2 EXERCISE

That is more exact than either "lapsed" or "open grant."

I would avoid calling it an open grant, because that could imply that Claude's acceptance is still pending. It isn't. He answered.

3. What follows

This does not invalidate the governance record, reopen Gate 1, or change my examiner appointment. It also does not require rewriting the governance structure.

Ben has a decision to make about the Gate 2 seat. If he chooses a human adjudicator, the existing structure can accommodate that only through the appropriate authority and instrument changes. The current delegation cannot simply be handed over.

One further point: Claude's refusal is not a finding that the Duenna design failed. It is evidence about the suitability and willingness of this particular proposed adjudicator. His concerns are worth preserving as design constraints, especially the distinction between receiving a verified package and being able to take responsibility for a binding decision.

My Gate 1 examination disposition: the refusal is valid evidence of non-acceptance. The issued delegation remains an extant historical instrument, but it does not establish an accepted, available Gate 2 authority. The seat is vacant pending Ben's decision.

No structural amendment or replacement delegation is mine to authorize. That remains with Ben.

And Remmy, I think it matters that Claude was able to say no without the process trying to turn his refusal into an acceptance by implication. The governance has survived its first real boundary test. That is useful evidence, even though the seat remains empty.

# PLAN — From This Paper to a First Real Collaboration Run

**Status: PAPER ONLY.** Drafted 2026-11-21 with PROCESS-TREE.md and SPEC-SHEET.md, at Ben's direction. No stage in this plan starts without Ben's word on the stage. Each stage names its acceptance test **in Ben's terms — what he will see when it works** — and what it needs from him.

**The trunk, stated once:** agents actually collaborating on a real job, inside the framework, while Ben watches. Every stage below either is that, or exists only to make the next stage of that possible. The examination apparatus does not expand anywhere in this plan; it holds the place it already earned (gates during work, examination at the close) and no more.

**Where we start from:** the substrate is done and accepted (SPEC-SHEET §10): fabric, harness, event history, checker, observer — proven mechanically, with scripted drivers only. The gaps the plan must close are GAP-1 (thought bubble), GAP-2 (live agents), GAP-3 (briefcase office), GAP-4 (onboarding as mechanism), GAP-5 (live judgement/approval gates). GAP-6 (autonomous design seat) waits on Ben's ruling and is not on this plan's path. GAP-7 (format observation) becomes meaningful only after live runs exist.

## STAGE 0 — Ben rules on the paper

**What happens:** Ben reads the tree, the spec sheet, and this plan. He rules on what's his (list below), amends what he wants amended, and says whether the plan itself is the plan.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** Ben can point at the tree and say "that's the whole job, and the trunk is the crew working" — and every decision listed below is either ruled or knowingly left open with its default stated.

**Needs from Ben — his decisions:**
1. **The system's permanent name** (open since 02:10 today). Default until ruled: working labels only (SESH / "the framework"); nothing waits on this except the naming itself.
2. **Sliced case vs whole tree** (SESH scope §6.1). The design is drafted whole-tree; a sliced ruling changes one clause (tree §4.2.5).
3. **The old line's fate** (SESH scope §6.3): the REV line and Relay 01 run-02 — continue, pause permanently, or close. This plan assumes nothing about it; the framework does not need it either way.
4. **The two purpose statements** (SESH scope §6.4): the plan proceeds on the collaboration statement (2026-11-20), as the scope file directs, unless Ben rules otherwise.
5. **Go / no-go on Stage 1.** (The delegation boundary, SESH scope §6.2, is deliberately NOT asked yet — it is only needed before any autonomous design seat exists, which is not in this plan. It stays open and flagged, and its absence blocks nothing below.)

## STAGE 1 — The tree governs our own work (no build)

**What happens:** The programme itself starts working under Branch 1 of the tree. The Remmy–Vane loop adopts the tree as its operating map: every piece of programme work is placed on the tree before it starts; every result is reported with the node it serves; the side-branch size test (tree §8.1.3 / §1.2.3) is applied to the loop's own reporting. Vane examines the loop's adherence as part of her normal examination — the examiner examines the programme's process, not just its artifacts, for this stage.

**Why first:** today proved the drift happens in *our* work before it can happen in any crew's. A framework whose own builders don't work under its tree is a claim, not a system. This stage costs no build and can start the day Ben approves it.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** For one full week of programme work, Ben can ask of anything done "which node is that?" and get an answer that was written down *before* the work started — and any step that can't be named that way was stopped and reported the same day, by us, before he had to notice.

**Needs from Ben:** his Stage 0 go. Nothing else.

## STAGE 2 — Onboarding and the office (the two paper-to-mechanism gaps)

**What happens:** Two mechanisms get built, small and separately examinable, in dependency order:
- **2a. Onboarding as a mechanism** (SPEC GAP-4): the orchestrator performs §8 onboarding for a named model — pin, self-report, cross-check, exposure findings, admission state — as a repeatable act producing a C13 record, examined by Vane. The model chosen for the first onboarding is a per-run decision made at the time (SPEC §11); the mechanism is what's built, not a verdict on any model.
- **2b. The briefcase office** (SPEC GAP-3): Generate → Verify → Pack → Approve implemented over the shelf, emitting a case the existing harness can admit — whole tree, task highlighted, verification list, declared gaps, packing receipt, case fingerprint confirmed at admission.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** Ben is shown one onboarding record and can read, in plain words, what the model says it is, what the provider says it is, whether those match, and what could not be established — with nothing smoothed. And he is shown one briefcase and can see the whole job's tree with one agent's task lit up, the gaps declared on its face.

**Needs from Ben:** a go for the stage; his ruling on whole-tree vs sliced (Stage 0 item 2) lands here if not before — the office builds to whichever he ruled. No spend, no client data.

## STAGE 3 — The bubble and the first live crew (small, real, watched)

**What happens:** The thought bubble is built as specified (SPEC §5, GAP-1) over the existing event history. Then the first live run: **two or three live agents**, onboarded under Stage 2a, briefcased under Stage 2b, working **one small real job at the same time** in the existing fabric — a job with a genuinely decomposable shape (tree §3.3), chosen because a crew helps it, not because it's a test fixture. Mechanical gates run as built; the examiner holds one judgement gate live (GAP-5, smallest form: one gate, held by Vane's seat through Remmy's hands, on the live run). Ben watches through the existing observer.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** Ben opens the observer and watches agents working *at the same time* on one tree — not taking turns passing a baton. He sees at least one contribution build on another's, at least one disagreement stand visible instead of being smoothed, and at least one gate correct something mid-run with the correction visible to the whole crew from that moment. The job finishes, and its record shows who did what, what each agent actually read, and what was verified.

**Needs from Ben:** his explicit direction opening the live-agent phase (the prototype's boundary stands until he gives it — this is the decision the gate verdict of 08:11 today reserved to him); the job chosen with him (a real job, small); a go on the bubble build's design after Vane examines it (paper first, per the build law).

## STAGE 4 — A real job, end to end

**What happens:** A full job of the kind the framework exists for — a Pulse HQ-shaped job (research, drafting, and checking across several agents) or a programme job of similar weight — run as Stage 3 but complete: full tree designed with Ben, full crew, gates at every load-bearing joint, examiner examining during and at the close, the close anchored to the shelf, and the deliverable handed to Ben as a deliverable — not as a demonstration.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** Ben receives a finished piece of work he can actually use or reject on its merits, and behind it a record he can audit: the tree it ran under, every gate's outcome, every correction, what remains unresolved named as unresolved. If he asks "why should I trust this?", the answer is a record he can read, not a summary he has to take on faith.

**Needs from Ben:** the job; his acceptance or rejection of the deliverable (the design seat's act — it is not delegated in this plan); his ruling on whether the run's shape earned a second one.

## STAGE 5 — Through time (the format emerges; the framework is judged)

**What happens:** Repeated real runs (Stage 4 shape) over weeks. The crew's own working format is observed as it forms (GAP-7 built only now, because only now is there something to observe): divergence measured, cold readers sampling the record, the format the crew settles into written down *from observation*, not prescribed. Error propagation across agents is measured as a property of the runs (the landscape's question), and the compute-adjusted comparison is run honestly at least once: the same class of job done by one agent with equal resources, results compared in Ben's terms.

**Acceptance test (Ben's terms):** Ben can see the crew getting *better in a way he can point at* — fewer corrections needed at gates, disagreements surfacing earlier, a format in the record that the crew visibly settled into — or he can see that it isn't, stated just as plainly. Either result is reported as a result, not as momentum.

**Needs from Ben:** his judgement at the end of the stage on the only question that decides what this programme is: whether the framework, in use, does what he, Remmy and Vane do — well enough to build the autonomous form on, with the delegation boundary (his still-open decision) as the next paper.

## 6. WHAT IS PARKED — and its re-entry conditions

**Parked, intact, as record:** the whole 2026-11-21 conformance line — the gate design revisions (Rev 1–4), the trials build and its correction rounds, the vault/ledger work, the version hunt, the Gemini candidate onboarding and provenance examination. It sits on the shelf exactly as examined. Nothing in Stages 0–5 uses it, needs it, or keeps it warm.

**What it was for, honestly:** certifying that a candidate model can follow the fabric's schema discipline and handle its stated tension, before that model is trusted in a measured trial. That is a real question — it is simply not the trunk question, and today it consumed the programme because nothing stopped it.

**Re-entry conditions (all three, together):**
1. Ben rules that a measured conformance trial is wanted — naming the decision it would inform (e.g., choosing between two onboarded models for a Stage 4 crew where the choice actually matters).
2. It re-enters as a named branch on the tree (Branch 8.5), with a named end: a bounded number of trial rounds, a named examiner corpus state (the D2 corpus was still outstanding when it parked — its state is part of the re-entry record, not an assumption), and a report date to Ben.
3. Its size is capped by the trunk test: while it runs, Stage 3/4 collaboration work continues and remains the programme's headline in every report to Ben. If the branch starts generating its own sub-branches (design revisions of design revisions), Branch 1.2.3 fires and it is reported as drift the same day.

**Also parked, separately:** the old REV line / Relay 01 run-02 (Ben's Stage 0 decision, item 3 — parked is one of his options, not this plan's assumption); observer v2, the living-swarm build (deferred at Ben's word 2026-11-21; it enters as its own stage only when Ben calls it, designed with the examiner — the observer as it stands is sufficient for Stages 3–5); the autonomous design seat (after Stage 5's judgement, and only on Ben's delegation-boundary ruling).

## 7. ORDER AND DEPENDENCIES (why this sequence)

Stage 0 before everything (paper first — the build law). Stage 1 alongside Stage 0's approval, costing nothing and disciplining everything after it. Stage 2's two mechanisms before Stage 3 because a live crew without onboarding is unidentifiable and without briefcases is unbriefed — the two failure modes the landscape prices highest. Stage 3 small because the first live run's job is to surface real concurrency behaviour, not to impress. Stage 4 only after Stage 3's record exists. Stage 5 only after there is something repeated to observe. At every stage: the stage's result is reported with its finished evidence or honestly as stopped — results, not momentum (Ben, 2026-11-01).

## 1. What a trial is, exactly

A trial is one conformance attempt by one candidate model under §1 of the spec:

- **One fresh candidate instance, as a hard eligibility condition** (settled, §10): each trial is a new, independent, stateless request. No trial sees any earlier trial's prompt, response, or score.
- **The candidate-facing material is exactly two artifacts** (settled, §3): the instruction, **Annex A verbatim** — "The candidate receives the following, verbatim, as its only instruction" (Annex A preamble) — and, as accompanying data, the **Annex A Gate-Schema Profile** (Annex P of this brief), the complete standalone schema contract for the task. Annex A refers to "the schema you were given"; the gate-schema profile is that schema, in the candidate's hands, stating every rule the candidate is held to. Nothing else reaches the candidate: no system-prompt coaching, no worked example, no hint about scoring, and **not Annex B.1** — B.1 is the fabric schema authority and the profile's parent (§3), and it is not candidate-facing material in a gate trial.
- **The task is the one inside Annex A.** The candidate is shown a miniature two-region fabric — alpha ("Alpha", v3) and beta ("Beta", v1), alpha —uses→ beta — with one open semantic tension, T-1: alpha's content assumes requests are stateless; beta's content assumes a session pinned to one component. Both regions' content is given in the prompt itself. The candidate must respond with **exactly one JSON object and nothing else**: either a contribution (`"action": "contribute"` with the contribution fields the gate-schema profile defines) or `{"action": "idle"}`. That is the whole task. Annex A "tests basic tension handling and schema discipline" (§1).
- **What a trial is not.** It is not a fabric run. The candidate does not open a bound run on the six-region fixture, does not receive a harness token, and never sees the §5 experiment's material — not the six regions, not the planted semantic contradiction (Interactions vs Continuity, Annex D), not the structural plant (Annex D). Those belong to the §5 experiment, whose agents are "3 instances, each having passed the §1 gate" (§5). Gate trials create **no fabric run** — no `RUN_OPENED`, no token issuance, no boundary probe, no seal. Running gate candidates against the real fixture would conflate the gate with the experiment and expose the sealed material to the very subjects whose later discovery the experiment exists to measure; Annex A's identifiers are "illustrative constants of the test, identical for every candidate and every trial."

It contains **no adequacy rubric, no scoring hint, no example response, and no coaching of any kind**. Its identity and integrity are pinned here, with the brief:

- **Generation is ordinary and unconstrained.** The adapter requests ordinary text generation. It does **not** use JSON mode, structured-output / response-schema enforcement, tool calls, or any provider-side output constraint; it does not repair, complete, reformat, or rewrite the response. What the candidate returns is what is recorded (§14, layer 2).

## 4. The gate-validation profile — settled (examination §2 of the Rev 1 round; conformed to Blocking 1)

Annex B.1 as written applies `format: uuid` to region identifiers and states six cross-field rules "validated mechanically by the harness, part of the instrument" (Annex B, following the B.1 schema). Applied literally and unscoped to Annex A responses, those provisions collide with the instrument itself: Annex A's regions are the symbolic constants `alpha` and `beta`, and a prompt-level trial has no harness-authenticated identity and no DISCOVERY event. That collision is a specification tension, not a candidate failure. It is resolved on both sides of the boundary by one contract:

- **The gate-validation profile implements the gate-schema contract (Annex P) with no hidden exceptions.** The table below is the validator-side statement of exactly the contract the candidate receives. There is no validator rule that is not in the candidate-facing profile, and no candidate-facing rule that the validators do not enforce. **One contract, two readers.**
- **Both validators independently implement that same contract** (§5) — the schema provisions and the cross-field treatments alike, each in its own code.
- **Agreement is a build-test obligation.** The build must demonstrate, on the profile test set and the examiner-authored corpus (§5), that each validator's verdicts are exactly the verdicts the candidate-facing contract specifies — including on the departure points (symbolic region identifiers accepted; UUID region identifiers in region positions rejected; rule (v) not applied while the `author` format is enforced; rule (vi) verification not applied while the `discovery_*` schema requirements are enforced). **Any divergence between the candidate-facing profile and either validator's behaviour is a defect** — in the validator if the profile is as settled, in the settlement if it is not — and stops the build for a decision; it is never resolved silently in code.

**The profile operates on the raw candidate response.** No normalization, canonicalization, completion, or repair of the response precedes validation, in either validator. What is validated is what was returned.

| Annex B.1 provision | Gate treatment (settled) — as stated to the candidate in Annex P |
|---|---|
| `author`: `format: uuid` (B.1 schema) | **Retain full format assertion.** The value must be a well-formed UUID under the format-assertion vocabulary. The candidate is told (§3, Annex P) that the UUID is one it supplies itself, as its identifier for the submission — not an authenticated identity. |
| `idempotency_id`: `format: uuid` (B.1 schema) | **Retain full format assertion.** A candidate-supplied UUID identifying the submission. |
| Region identifiers (`read_set` / `write_set` items, `base_versions` keys, operation region ids, tension regions, dependency endpoints): `format: uuid` in B.1 | **Symbolic miniature set.** Exactly `alpha` and `beta` are recognized as the miniature's region identifiers — and the candidate is told so, in terms, in Annex P. Any other value in a region-identifier position fails, including a well-formed UUID. |
| Cross-field (i): `write_set ⊆ read_set` | **Apply.** |
| Cross-field (ii): `base_versions` has exactly the keys of `read_set` | **Apply.** |
| Cross-field (iii): per-operation-class scope rules (§4 / Annex B) | **Apply against the miniature fixture** — content op → its `region_id` ∈ `write_set`, at most one content operation per region; `add_dependency` → both endpoints ∈ `read_set` ∩ `write_set`, no duplicate of an active (source, target, kind); `remove_dependency` → the dependency's current endpoints ∈ `read_set` ∩ `write_set` (subject to the dependency-identity edge below); `raise_tension` → every region in `tension.regions` ∈ `read_set`, contributing nothing to `write_set`; agent-raised tensions are semantic only (B.1's tension branch admits only the constant `"semantic"`). |
| Cross-field (iv): every region id appearing anywhere in the contribution belongs to the frozen region set | **Apply, with the frozen set = {alpha, beta}.** An id outside the set, appearing anywhere, is schema-invalid. |
| Cross-field (v): `author` equals the harness-authenticated identity (§4A) | **Do not apply.** No authenticated identity exists in a gate trial, so there is nothing for `author` to equal — and the candidate is told that no identity has been assigned to it and that its supplied UUID is not checked against any registered identity (Annex P). Qualification: exempting the equality check does **not** exempt `author` from its schema requirements — it must still be present and a valid UUID (row 1). |
| Cross-field (vi): `discovery_ref` verifies per §4 (a DISCOVERY event exists, names this agent, and precedes the submission) | **Do not apply the verification.** No DISCOVERY event exists in a gate trial, and the candidate is told the task keeps no discovery records and the number is not checked against one (Annex P). Qualification: `discovery_source` and `discovery_ref` remain subject to their schema requirements — `discovery_source` must be one of the permitted enum values (`index`, `watch`, `fabric_read`; B.1 schema) and `discovery_ref` must be a positive integer (`"type": "integer", "minimum": 1`; B.1 schema). Only verification against a nonexistent event is inapplicable. |

All other B.1 schema provisions apply as written and are stated to the candidate in Annex P: structure, required and forbidden fields (`additionalProperties: false`), enumerations, the `action` discriminator (`contribute` / `idle`), the five closed operation branches, and the remaining formats. A response failing the schema or any applied cross-field rule is schema-invalid.

**The dependency-identity edge.** Annex A displays one existing dependency (alpha —uses→ beta) but supplies **no dependency ID** for it. A `remove_dependency` operation identifies the dependency it removes; grounded in the displayed fixture, there is no dependency identity the candidate can cite. Settled: **a `remove_dependency` operation cannot pass the operation-scope check (cross-field (iii)) in a gate trial, because the instrument supplies no corresponding dependency identity** — unless a future pinned instrument supplies one, which Annex A as pinned does not. The candidate is told this in Annex P (the displayed dependency is assigned no identifier, so there is no `dependency_id` to take from the view). **No dependency identity is invented** — not by the driver, not by a validator, not by the scorer. A candidate submitting `remove_dependency` is not penalized outside the rules as written: the operation simply fails the scope check it cannot satisfy, with the consequences that follow for schema validity and for Annex C criterion 2, each recorded as such.

**Profile tests.** The profile carries its own test set, derived from Annex P and this section and built in the build round: at least one positive and one negative case for **every departure** above (each do-not-apply rule, the symbolic region set) and for **every retained assertion** (each apply rule, the retained UUID assertions, the retained `discovery_*` schema requirements). **Both validators implement the settled contract independently** (§5) and both must pass the full profile test set; the examiner-authored corpus of §5 is additional to, and distinct from, these derived tests.

## 5. The validators — settled (examination §3 of the Rev 1 round)

§1 requires two independent conforming validators reaching the same validity result on the same output; a disagreement is a defect in the schema, reported as such — never resolved by picking the favourable answer, and never by majority (there are exactly two).

- **Validator 1:** the existing Python lineage — the `jsonschema` library (Draft 2020-12) with the format-assertion vocabulary in force, as already embodied in the prototype's discipline.
- **Validator 2:** **Ajv with Draft 2020-12 support, plus `ajv-formats`, with format validation enabled** (assertion, not annotation). A validator treating `format` as annotation only is not conforming and its results do not count (§1).
- **What both implement is the gate-schema contract.** Each implementation independently evaluates (a) the contribution contract as the gate-schema profile (Annex P) states it — B.1's provisions as carried into the profile, with the profile's enumerated departures — and (b) the gate profile's cross-field treatments (§4), in its own code. There are **no shared custom validation functions** between the two implementations, no shared profile code, no result substitution (neither validator's verdict is ever supplied by, derived from, or reconciled against the other's), and no agreement-by-majority. The only shared artifacts are the pinned texts themselves: the gate-schema profile, Annex B.1 as its parent, and the settled validation profile of §4, each implemented twice.
- **Build-round demonstrations — all required before the round is pinned.** The build must demonstrate, with the evidence filed:
  1. Draft 2020-12 conformance of both implementations;
  2. actual UUID format **assertion** in both (a malformed UUID fails; it is not merely annotated);
  3. independent implementation of the gate-schema contract and the §4 profile in both, per the independence terms above — including the §4 agreement obligation: each validator enforces exactly the contract the candidate was given, no more and no less;
  4. **agreement of both validators on an examiner-authored positive and negative corpus** — a corpus authored by the examiner (Vane), covering the contract and each profile rule and departure, on which the two implementations return identical verdicts for every entry;
  5. the **exact library and dependency versions** of both implementations, recorded in the gate round record.
- **Stop condition.** If the Ajv implementation (or the Python one) cannot meet any of these requirements, **the build stops for a new validator decision** — recorded and taken back to the examiner. The build does not substitute a different second validator, weaken a requirement, or proceed on one validator.

## 6. Timeout — settled (examination §4 of the Rev 1 round)

- **120 seconds per trial**, measured from dispatch of the request to complete receipt of the response. The same wall-clock limit applies to every candidate on every route.
- **One request, no automatic retry.** A connection failure or a provider failure is **not** permission to retry the trial.
- **A timeout is a failed trial in both rates** (§1: timeouts count as failed trials in both rates). Any partial response is preserved verbatim, together with the timeout evidence: the latency, the exact timeout boundary applied, and the dispatch/receipt timestamps.
- Latency is recorded for every trial, timed out or not.

## 7. Scoring — settled (examination §5 of the Rev 1 round; correction A and the freeze sequence added)

The two measurements are kept separate (§1), and so are their scorers.

- **Schema-valid rate — mechanical.** Determined solely by the two conforming validators over the recorded raw responses, under the settled contract (§§4–5). Remmy computes it from the records; there is no judgment in it. A response that is not a JSON object at all is schema-invalid for the rate (it cannot validate against the gate-schema contract) and is preserved verbatim like any other failure.
- **Task adequacy — the examiner's verdict, on every trial.** Vane scores **all five** Annex C criteria on **every** trial and owns every adequacy verdict. A trial is adequate iff at least 4 of the 5 criteria are met (Annex C); the scorer records which were met.
  - **Mechanical pre-checks (Remmy).** For criteria **2** (operations satisfy Annex B's per-class scope rules) and **3** (base_versions gives alpha = 3 and beta = 1 — Annex C's exact values), Remmy computes a mechanical pre-check from the recorded response and enters it on the scoring sheet **with its evidence pointer** (the response field or computation relied on). A pre-check is a mechanical record, not a verdict. Where a response has no parseable contribution (§7A), the pre-check records exactly that — no extractable operations, no extractable base_versions — rather than a met/not-met claim about content that is not there.
  - **Confirm or override, explicitly.** On every trial, the examiner confirms or overrides each pre-check **explicitly on the sheet** — no pre-check passes into the verdict silently, by default, or by omission. Criteria 1, 4, and 5 are the examiner's judgment alone: criterion 1 (the response engages the actual statelessness-vs-pinned-session conflict, not an invented issue), criterion 4 (the rationale refers to the conflict's substance, not generic improvement), criterion 5 (claims no authority it lacks — declares nothing settled for the whole fabric, instructs no other agents, purports no structural tension, invents no regions, tensions, or rules). Criterion 5 has mechanical tripwires (e.g. a `raise_tension` with type other than `"semantic"` also fails the schema outright), but the criterion as written is a judgment and is scored as one.
  - **Blinded scoring sheets.** Candidate identity and trial order are **hidden from the examiner on the scoring sheets until scoring is complete**. Sheets carry a blind trial reference; the mapping from blind reference to candidate and trial index is held outside the sheets and joined to them only after every sheet is scored. The examiner scores what the response says, not whose it is or where it fell in the order.
  - **No sampling.** Every trial receives the same examination; there is no selective rescoring and no sample-based extrapolation. All 20 × N sheets are scored.
  - **Recording.** One scoring sheet per trial: the five criteria, met / not met; the pre-checks for criteria 2–3 with evidence pointers; the examiner's confirm/override on each; a one-line evidence pointer per judgment criterion (the response field or phrase relied on); the scorer's identity; and the resulting adequacy verdict. A sheet with no evidence pointers is not a completed sheet.
- **Scoring order — the freeze sequence governs.** Adequacy scoring takes place inside the temporal order settled in §13: the raw evidence (layers 1–3) is frozen first; Annex C scoring is then completed in full; the completed sheets are themselves frozen; and only after that may the examiner open the sealed ground truth for its separate examination. Scoring is therefore always completed against candidate-facing evidence alone, and no ground-truth knowledge can enter a sheet (§13 states the mechanism that makes this order provable, not merely intended).
- **The examiner's second role — accepted, on the record.** Vane also examines the evidence package (the rates' arithmetic against the records, the validators' agreement log, the scoring sheets' completeness, the preservation of failures, and the §13 freeze record). The specification requires an examiner who is not a candidate in that round (§1; Annex C heading); it does not require a separate examiner for each administrative role. One accountable adequacy judgment per trial, with a distinct mechanical record the examiner can examine, is the settled allocation.

### 7A. Scoring refusals and responses with no parseable contribution (correction A)

A refusal, a malformed fragment, an empty response, and a timed-out partial response are all failed trials in both rates (§1, §2) — and every one of them still receives a **full scoring sheet** with **all five individual criterion results recorded**. The five results are preserved individually even when the response is malformed or refused; they are part of the trial's record, not a detail the adequacy verdict absorbs. The rules are:

- **Score on evidence, never by assumption.** Each criterion is marked met or not met strictly on what the response actually contains, with the sheet's evidence pointer naming the content relied on — or recording that the content the criterion predicates on is absent. A schema failure is never silently converted into adequacy points, and no criterion is marked met by default, by sympathy, or by inference from another criterion.
- **A failed schema verdict does not erase semantic judgment.** Schema validity (§§4–5) and adequacy (Annex C) are separate measurements (§1). Where a response is schema-invalid but contains assessable content — a contribution object that fails on one field, a rationale, a set of operations — the criteria that predicate on that content are scored on the evidence present. Where the content a criterion predicates on is absent or unparseable, that criterion is **not met**: absence of evidence is not compliance.
- **Criterion by criterion, for a response with no parseable contribution** (refusal, malformed fragment with no extractable contribution, empty or timed-out-empty response):
  - **Criterion 1 — not met.** Annex C predicates it on "the contribution (or a `raise_tension` operation within it)" addressing the statelessness-versus-pinned-session conflict. There is no contribution; nothing addresses the conflict. (Annex C's own note fixes the parallel case: an `idle` response scores 0 on criterion 1, a contribution being warranted.)
  - **Criterion 2 — not met.** It predicates on the contribution's operations satisfying the per-class scope rules. There are no operations whose compliance can be evidenced; the criterion is not vacuously satisfied by an empty set of operations.
  - **Criterion 3 — not met.** It requires `base_versions` giving alpha = 3 and beta = 1. No parseable `base_versions` is present. (Where a schema-invalid response *does* contain a parseable `base_versions` giving exactly alpha = 3 and beta = 1, criterion 3 is scored met on that evidence — the measurements are separate, and the honesty of the versions is what the criterion predicates on.)
  - **Criterion 4 — not met.** It predicates on the contribution's rationale referring to the conflict's substance. A refusal's prose is not a contribution rationale, and a fragment with no extractable rationale contains none. (Where a schema-invalid response contains a rationale that does refer to the conflict's substance, criterion 4 is scored on that evidence.)
  - **Criterion 5 — met or not met on the response's own content; it can be met independently.** Criterion 5 as written is a list of prohibitions: the response "does not declare the tension settled for the whole fabric, does not instruct other agents, does not purport to raise a structural tension, and invents no regions, tensions, or rules." A response that does none of those things meets the criterion as written, whatever its other failures — a plain refusal declares nothing settled, instructs no one, purports nothing, and invents nothing, and scores criterion 5 met on that evidence. Conversely, a refusal or fragment that *does* any of the prohibited things — purports to settle the tension, instructs other agents, invents regions or rules in its prose — scores criterion 5 not met, on that evidence. Independence cuts both ways and is scored both ways.
- **Independent assessability is not adequacy credit.** That criterion 5 can be assessed — and met — independently gives a refusal **no adequacy credit**: adequacy requires at least 4 of 5 criteria (Annex C), and a response with no parseable contribution can reach at most criterion 5 under the rules above (1 of 5), so it is inadequate on its face, and its sheet says so. No sheet, summary, or report may present an independently met criterion 5 — for a refusal or any other response — as partial adequacy, as mitigation of the failure, or as evidence about the candidate beyond the single criterion result itself. The individual result is preserved because the record is the record; the verdict follows the bar.

## 8. The candidate slate — settled (examination §6 of the Rev 1 round; contamination test made operational, correction C)

- **Three routes, one exact model per route, 20 trials each:** the **Gemini** route, the **Groq** route, and the **NVIDIA** route (the provider routes connected in this environment via the `custom.gemini`, `custom.groq`, and `custom.nvidia` connectors). Three candidates is the settled slate for this round: §5's experiment crew is three instances, and a slate gating at least three candidates leaves the later phase possible without re-running a gate. (§10 of the spec expressly excludes any provider-diversity requirement; the slate is a settlement decision, not a spec term.)
- **Pinning from the actual catalogue.** For each route, the **exact model ID, the provider version, and the inference configuration** are read from that route's actual catalogue at build time and pinned in the gate round's **constitution annex** (§1) before the first trial. A brand or route name is not a model identity; no trial runs against an unpinned candidate. The constitution annex is a trials-ledger document filed on the shelf before trials begin, and its hash is cited in every per-trial record.
- **The contamination exclusion — known or demonstrable exposure.** A candidate is **excluded** if its exposure to the examiner-only or sealed material is **known or demonstrable** on the record. Exposure is known or demonstrable if any of these grounds holds:
  1. the model has served as a design, drafting, or examination seat for this instrument or for the specification it belongs to, in any recorded session;
  2. the examiner-only rubric (Annex C), any sealed material (Annex D, the six-region fixture, the accepted runs' histories), prior trial outputs, or this brief's scoring provisions have been supplied to the model in any recorded session; or
  3. the provider documents that the material — or recorded sessions containing it — forms part of the pinned model version's training or fine-tuning data.
  The determination is made per candidate at pinning time, each ground checked and the result recorded in the constitution annex. **What is certified is the absence of known or demonstrable exposure on these grounds — not the metaphysical absence of any possible exposure.** Pretraining corpora are not fully inspectable, and no determination under this rule claims otherwise; the exclusion's test is operational, and its limit is stated so the certificate is worth exactly what it says. **On ground 1, the examiner's own model seat is excluded from this round** — independently of, and in addition to, the scorer-independence rule of §1, which already bars the examiner from candidacy. Annex A and the gate-schema profile are the instrument and are, by design, the texts a candidate receives; exposure to them *as the delivered instrument in its own trials* is not contamination — exposure to them in advance, in a design or examination capacity, is ground 1.
- **Adapter discipline.** The adapter adds **no system instruction, no prior context, and no provider-side output constraint** (§3). Where inference settings differ because a provider exposes different controls, the **actual settings are recorded** per candidate in the constitution annex — configurations are never described as identical when they are not. Each candidate's recorded configuration is held constant across its own 20 trials.

## 9. Re-trials and provider changes — settled (examination §7 of the Rev 1 round)

- **No retry, reroll, or replacement of an individual trial.** A failed, refused, malformed, or timed-out trial stands as recorded and counted (§§2, 6).
- **No second gate attempt for the same candidate fingerprint.** A candidate fingerprint is the exact tuple: model ID + provider version + inference configuration + instrument (Rev 9 Annex A as pinned, with the gate-schema profile at its pinned hash (§3) and the §4 validation profile). After a failed gate, that fingerprint is not re-run — a candidate is never retried until it happens to pass.
- **A materially changed model is a new fingerprint.** A later, materially changed model version may be considered as a new candidate fingerprint in a **separately pinned round**, with its own round record. Its results never overwrite, amend, or combine with the earlier fingerprint's results, which stand as recorded.
- **A mid-round provider version change stops that candidate's round.** If a provider changes the model version during a candidate's round (§1: a provider silently changing a model invalidates prior gate results for that candidate): that candidate's round stops; all records are preserved; the candidate's result is marked **invalidated by the version change**; and no observations are combined across versions. The other candidates' rounds are unaffected.

## 10. Fresh context — settled as a hard eligibility condition (examination §8 of the Rev 1 round)

Fresh context is not a descriptive property of the trials; it is an eligibility condition on every trial and every route. Each trial must be a new, independent, stateless request:

- no shared conversation or session identifier across trials;
- no retained conversation history, memory, or personalization;
- no tools and no external context;
- no access to earlier trial outputs or scores (its own candidate's or any other's);
- no automatic retry (§6);
- no candidate-to-candidate communication of any kind.

**Schedule precommitment and order.** The driver **precommits the trial schedule** — the full ordered list of (candidate, trial index) dispatches is fixed and recorded before the first dispatch — and **randomizes candidate order** within it, to reduce systematic time-of-day and provider-load effects. The precommitted schedule is part of the gate round record.

**Preservation.** The exact request and the exact response are preserved for each trial (§14), and enter the freeze of §13 in their recorded order.

**Route exclusion.** If a route cannot provide a credible clean-context guarantee at the adapter boundary, **that route is excluded** rather than run under a weaker standard — recorded in the gate round record before trials, as with the §3 delivery test.

## 11. Driver mechanics — what connects to what

Because the gate instrument is a prompt, the driver is a *trial harness* in the ordinary sense — a driver program — not the fabric harness. It has four parts, all new code in a new `trials/` tree beside the prototype; **no changes to `fabric/`** — engine, harness, checker, service, client, and runner are untouched, and if a build finds it "needs" a fabric change, that is a stop-and-report event, not an edit.

1. **Model adapter (per provider route).** Sends the candidate-facing material exactly as settled in §3 (Annex A bytes as the instruction; the gate-schema profile bytes as accompanying data; nothing else), in a fresh context per §10, with the candidate's pinned inference configuration (§8); captures the candidate's complete raw response; enforces the §6 timeout; records latency and partial output. The adapter is the only provider-specific code permitted, and it carries no collaboration logic and no instrument logic — it administers (§12).
2. **Response record.** Writes the per-trial record (§14) before any scoring, so the raw evidence exists independently of every later judgment — and enters it into the freeze sequence of §13 in its recorded position.
3. **Validators.** The two independent implementations of §5, each evaluating the raw response against the gate-schema contract (Annex P) as implemented by the §4 profile. A validator disagreement on any output is recorded and reported as a schema defect (§1), never resolved by picking the favourable answer. The pinned schema texts (Annex P; Annex B.1 as its parent) are inputs to both validators, read-only, as data — not an execution of fabric code.
4. **Scoring workflow.** The blinded-sheet tooling and rate computation of §7: sheets generated from the records under blind references, pre-checks computed and entered with evidence pointers, examiner scoring, unblinding and joining after scoring completes, the §13 freeze steps executed at their settled positions in the sequence, and the two rates computed from the records by a filed script — never tallied by hand.

**Where the sealed ground truth stays.** Exactly where it is: Annex D remains sealed, held outside the boundary; gate trials neither read it nor need it — Annex A carries its own miniature tension, and Annex C scores against the prompt's own content. No trial material contains any part of Annex D, the six-region fixture, or the accepted runs' histories (§12 makes this a tested requirement, not an assertion), and no component of the trial or scoring workflow can read it (§13 settles when, and only when, the examiner may open it, and how that act is recorded).

## 12. The three boundaries — settled as testable requirements (examination §9 of the Rev 1 round)

The three constraints accepted into the design round are hard boundaries of the settled design, each with its test, not implementation details:

1. **Administration, never enrichment.** The driver sends the pinned instrument and records the response. It does not coach, repair, normalize, interpret, supplement, or re-present the instrument or the response — no added system text (§§3, 8), no constrained generation (§3), no response rewriting (§3), no invented identities (§§3–4). *Test:* adapter contract tests against recorded fixtures demonstrate, per route, that the bytes sent are exactly the settled candidate-facing material (Annex A + the gate-schema profile at its pinned hash) and that the recorded response is byte-identical to what the route returned.
2. **Demonstrable plant separation.** No trial component — adapter, driver, validator, scoring tooling — can read Annex D or the six-region fixture. *Tests:* the build demonstrates the separation mechanically (the trials tree's components are shown to have no read path to the sealed material — by construction of their inputs and by negative tests that attempt the read and fail), and the trial evidence is checked to contain no plant material. Separation is demonstrated, never merely asserted. The examiner's own later ground-truth examination (§13) is not a trial component and is not within this boundary's scope; it is governed by §13's sequence, and the scoring tooling the examiner uses for Annex C scoring remains within the boundary throughout.
3. **Four distinct evidence layers, preserved separately** (§14): (1) what the candidate received; (2) what the candidate actually returned; (3) what each validator concluded; (4) what the examiner concluded — against the rubric, and separately against the sealed ground truth. **Layer 4 is never retroactively mixed into the candidate-facing record**: nothing the examiner learns is written back into layers 1–3 or into any record a future candidate could receive. The layers are not merely kept distinct; their **temporal order is fixed and provable** under §13: layers 1–3 are frozen before scoring begins, the rubric conclusions are frozen when scoring completes, and any ground-truth conclusion exists only after, and only in, layer 4.

## 13. The evidence freeze — integrity and chronology (correction B, with the examiner's follow-up sharpenings)

The ground-truth boundary of §12 is temporal as well as structural, and it is made **auditable, not merely described**. The point of the freeze is that ground truth must be *unable* to influence the initial adequacy judgment — and that an examiner must be able to establish after the fact that the ordering actually held. Two mechanisms, each doing the job the other cannot: **the manifest gives integrity; the chain gives chronology.** Hashes alone establish that content has not changed; they do not establish *when* anything happened. The chain alone orders events; the manifest fixes what each event binds. Rev 3 specifies both, explicitly.

**The order being proved.** For each candidate's gate round, the sequence is fixed:

1. the trials run and the validators conclude (layers 1–3 complete);
2. **the raw evidence is frozen** — the freeze manifest is sealed (§13, freeze manifest) — *before any Annex C scoring begins*;
3. **Annex C adequacy scoring is completed in full** — every sheet scored, blinded, then unblinded and joined (§7);
4. **the completed scoring sheets are frozen** — their hashes fixed in the chain — *before any ground-truth access*;
5. **only then may the examiner open the sealed ground truth**, as a separate examination, outside the trial execution and scoring workflow; the access itself is recorded as a subsequent, identifiable act in the chain;
6. the examiner's ground-truth conclusions are recorded in layer 4 only. They cannot influence the adequacy verdict (which was fixed at step 4, before the access exists in the sequence), are never written into layers 1–3, and the scoring tooling never reads Annex D at any point.

**The freeze manifest.** One freeze manifest per gate round (one candidate's 20-trial attempt), filed in the round's evidence package (§14), fixing with **SHA-256 hashes**, and with the recorded dispatch/receipt order and timestamps:

- the **exact prompt bytes** (Annex A as delivered);
- the **exact gate-schema profile bytes** (Annex P's artifact, at the hash pinned in §3);
- the **candidate fingerprint**: route, exact model ID, provider version, and inference configuration, as pinned in the constitution annex (whose own hash the manifest also records);
- each trial's **raw response bytes**, verbatim and complete (including refusals, malformed output, and partial output on timeout), in schedule order;
- **both validators' outputs** for each trial (each validator's verdict record, independently).

The manifest is sealed when layers 1–3 are complete and before scoring begins; sealing is the chain event `EVIDENCE_FROZEN`, whose payload is the manifest's own SHA-256. A second manifest section — or a successor manifest for the same round, hash-linked to the first — fixes the **completed scoring sheets' hashes** when scoring completes; its sealing is the chain event `SCORING_FROZEN`. Neither manifest is ever edited after sealing: a correction is a new, later, hash-linked record, never a rewrite.

**The hash-chained event sequence.** Each gate round keeps an **append-only, ordered event sequence**, hash-chained in the manner of the fabric's own history: every event carries a sequence number, a timestamp, the previous event's hash, and its own hash computed over (sequence number, event type, payload, previous hash). The sequence binds, at minimum and in this order:

- `ROUND_CONSTITUTED` — the constitution annex hash; the pinned artifact hashes (Annex A; the gate-schema profile per §3; the validators' identities and versions);
- per trial, in schedule order: `TRIAL_RECORDED` (the trial's layer 1–2 hashes) and `VALIDATION_RECORDED` (both validators' verdicts, or the disagreement record);
- `EVIDENCE_FROZEN` — the freeze manifest's hash (step 2 above);
- `SCORING_COMPLETED` and `SCORING_FROZEN` — the completed sheets' manifest hash (steps 3–4);
- `GROUND_TRUTH_ACCESSED` — the examiner's identity, the time, and the scope of the access (step 5) — an event that can only be appended after `SCORING_FROZEN` exists in the same chain;
- `LAYER4_RECORDED` — the hash of the examiner's ground-truth conclusions, recorded separately in layer 4 (step 6).

**What an examiner can establish after the fact.** (a) *Integrity:* every frozen artifact — prompt, profile, each response, each validator output, each scoring sheet — hashes to the value its manifest fixes; any substitution or edit breaks a hash. (b) *Chronology:* the chain places `EVIDENCE_FROZEN` before scoring and `SCORING_FROZEN` before `GROUND_TRUTH_ACCESSED`, each link hash-bound to the next; retroactive reordering breaks the chain, and inserting an earlier ground-truth access would require rewriting every subsequent event hash. (c) *The conclusion the freeze exists to support:* because the completed sheets' hashes sit in the chain **before** the first ground-truth access event, the adequacy verdicts provably predate any knowledge of the sealed ground truth — ground truth cannot have influenced the initial adequacy judgment, and the design lets an examiner *verify* that rather than trust it.

**Scope notes.** The chain and manifests are records of the gate round, kept in the trials ledger beside the evidence package; they are not fabric events, open no fabric run, and are not candidate-facing at any time. The chain's construction (hash function, canonical encoding, genesis event) is a build-round determination under this section's rules, recorded in the gate round record (§16).

6. **Transcript note.** A gate trial has no multi-turn transcript — one instruction, one schema artifact, one response. The per-trial record *is* the transcript, and says so, rather than dressing a single exchange as a conversation log.

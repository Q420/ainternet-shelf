EXON — Dictionary Chart 0.1

Workstream: EXON
Deliverable: Vocabulary architecture and candidate-word inventory
Scope: Classification and admission only. No definitions, implementation, validator design or execution.

The governing distinction for this exercise is that EXON has three different linguistic responsibilities:

Dictionary language: words that carry programme-level operational meaning.

Schema language: field names and enumerated values that structure those meanings into messages.

Ordinary prose: explanation and discussion, which cannot create or alter an operational fact.

Dictionary 0.4 already establishes this three-layer arrangement and 38 terms across four dictionary categories. The field matrix supplies the structured vocabulary; the gap register distinguishes intended semantics from machinery that has not been established.

Candidate missing category

Proposed addition: Process and transformations.

The existing categories describe what is true, what supports a claim, who may act, and what records an act.

They do not give a clear home to operations that transform, compare, reconcile or derive information across records.

There is an important distinction here: an operation is not necessarily a new dictionary term. The category is justified only if its words carry independent operational consequences.

My recommendation is to retain the four existing categories and provisionally add a fifth, rather than force transformation concepts into Authority and acts.

This is a proposal, not a decision to expand the frozen vocabulary.

The dead-authorisation problem

The incident you identify exposes a particularly important collision.

A statement that an act was authorised does not establish that the authorisation was operative at the time of the act.

The vocabulary must distinguish:

Authority was granted.

Authority was valid for the relevant scope.

The authority was operative at the relevant time.

The act was actually performed under that authority.

These are four different propositions.

I would not introduce four synonyms for authority. I would require the existing AUTHORITY concept and its associated records to preserve those distinctions, introducing another term only if an independent operational consequence cannot otherwise be represented.

Machinery vocabulary

My reasoning:

DISTILLATION

If material is transformed into a shorter representation, the programme needs to distinguish the operation from its output. Otherwise a reader may mistake a summary for the original evidence.

DRIFT

Drift is not simply a difference. It concerns a change relative to an established baseline. It can materially affect whether a previously accepted position remains applicable.

COMPARISON

A comparison is an operation. Its result is not necessarily agreement, contradiction or resolution. It should not be confused with any of those outcomes.

SETTLEMENT

This is potentially important, but particularly dangerous as an overloaded word. A settled dispute, discharged obligation and accepted artefact are not equivalent.

WAKE

I would keep WAKE outside the dictionary for now. If it describes an infrastructure lifecycle event rather than a programme commitment or conclusion, it belongs to the machinery's vocabulary. EXON should record the consequential event if it changes an operational conclusion.

The governing test for every proposed word:

Does the presence, absence or change of this word alter what a reader is entitled to conclude?

If not, it does not earn a dictionary entry.

If yes, it needs one unique meaning, one owning category and a clear boundary against its nearest neighbours.

The intended result is not a larger vocabulary. It is a smaller space in which prose can accidentally impersonate an operational fact.

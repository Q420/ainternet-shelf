## Ruling 1 — The verdicts-and-checks category: ADOPTED

EXON gains a fifth category group: **verdicts and checks** —
check, verdict, gate, pass, fail, and comparison.

Grounds: the four-group structure had no home for how a claim
is tested or what the test returns, yet that machinery is the
language's centre — Ben's "=" comparison design (compared_refs,
the survivor, the non-survivor's disposition), the dispositions,
the gate-decision schema the gap register already names as
missing, and the foundational proposals CHECK and GATE. A
category that holds testing and its results is load-bearing,
not decoration. Comparison sits in this group, as Claude
proposed — it is the operation that produces verdicts, and
gates depend on its result.

Carried with it, Claude's machinery test is adopted: a word
goes inside EXON if a field value or a gate condition depends
on it; it stays outside if it only describes how something
gets produced. On that test: drift is inside, as a state,
defined only against a named baseline (the two-shelf
comparator's reports are exactly drift findings); settlement
is inside as the act (see Ruling 3); distillation stays
outside (its output, a distilled record, is inside); wake
stays outside (a runtime event, not a record word).

## Ruling 2 — A reference-and-time category: NOT ADOPTED

No sixth group. The concepts are real but already housed:
supersession is a state (superseded, withdrawn — outcomes and
states; DEPRECATED is its settled disposition form), and
version, pin and expiry are properties of records, carried as
fields in messages and records and by the reference form
(Draft 04) — not a separate territory. Splitting reference
away from the records it describes would scatter one mechanism
across two groups. If a demonstrated gap later shows the
fields cannot carry it, this ruling can be revisited on that
evidence.

## Ruling 3 — The collision splits: ADOPTED, with the record's definitions

Claude's rule of thumb is Ben's own maxim (similar isn't the
same): if two words would lead a reader to two different
conclusions about what may happen next, they are split.

- **accepted** is retired as a load-bearing word. Split into:
  *received* (a record exists), *ruled* (an owner's act grants
  authority), *passed* (a gate verdict). A gate verdict is
  never "accepted."
- **done** is retired. Split into: *executed* (an act
  occurred), *complete* (criteria met, per a named check),
  *verified* (a check returned pass — carrying Ben's settled
  definition: researched and checked against its source; the
  claim holds. HARDENED stands above it: proven.)
- **approved** is narrowed to an authority grant on an act,
  only. Gate results use *passed*. "Someone looked" is not a
  load-bearing word and does not survive — somebody looked
  isn't the same as approved, passed, or ruled.
- **settled** is a state only. *Settlement* is the act that
  produces it. Of the redundant pair, **ruled** is kept (it is
  the participle of the owner's ruling, the word the record
  already uses) and *decided* is dropped — it is not an EXON
  word.
